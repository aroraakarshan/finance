## 3. Is the interest after Jul 2024 taxable while you work at Microsoft?

The law (AF Sch XI Part A):
- Para 2(g): "accumulated balance due" = the balance, or the part you can claim under the fund's
  rules, on the day you cease to be an employee of the employer maintaining the fund.
- Para 6, s.7(1)(a), s.16(i): yearly interest of an "employee participating in" a recognised fund is
  income only above the notified rate. Otherwise not taxed while you are a member-employee.
- Para 8(1)(a) and 8(2): the accumulated balance is exempt after 5 years' continuous service,
  counting earlier employers whose balance was transferred. Para 8(1)(c): exempt to the extent it
  is transferred to the new employer's fund. Sch II Sl.4 gives the exemption.
- Para 5(3)(b)-(c): a fund may keep a leaver's balance and add interest; a new fund may receive
  "any amount and interest thereof" from the former employer's fund.
- s.2(91): an EPFO scheme fund counts as a recognised fund.

| Argument | Points to |
|---|---|
| You are still "an employee participating in" the same EPFO fund, through Microsoft | exempt |
| On a job change EPFO only allows transfer, not a final payout, so nothing was "claimable" in Jul 2024 under para 2(g) (EPF Scheme para 69(2), 2-month rule: not re-read) | exempt |
| The balance must end in the Microsoft account; para 5(3)(c) and 8(2) treat the moved amount with its interest and earlier service as one | exempt |
| EPFO treats this interest as tax-free: no TDS, nothing in AIS (section 6) | exempt (practice) |
| Para 2(g) fixes the balance on the day you left Qualcomm; read literally, interest after that is not part of it | taxable |
| Ranjrekar (ITAT 2017): Wipro trust fund, retired 1 Apr 2002, withdrew 2011; interest after retirement "cannot be said ... qua an employee", taxed each year as other income. Karnataka HC 2018 upheld only the exempt part; the interest point was not before it | taxable, but for a retiree with no new job |

Conclusion: exempt on the better reading, because you never stopped being an employee in the EPF
and the money can only move to your new account. Confidence: medium (about 60-65%): the literal
para 2(g) reading and Ranjrekar give the department an argument, and no court has ruled on a
job-changer. After the transfer the question ends: interest is then on the Microsoft account.

## 5. Transferring to the Microsoft account: no tax

- Para 8(1)(c): the balance moved to the new employer's recognised fund is excluded from income;
  moving money between two accounts of the same fund is not a payment to you.
- The EPFO keeps the taxable/non-taxable split when it moves (PIB 25 Apr 2025, per `income/pay.md`).
- No TDS on a transfer (AF Sch XI para 10 applies only to taxable payouts). Check after the move
  that the Microsoft passbook shows the full Rs 28.97 L plus 2026-27 interest.

## 6. Rs 2.5 L limit on your own contributions

- Limit: interest on your own contributions above Rs 2.5 L a year (from 1 Apr 2021) is taxable
  (Sch II Sl.4 condition; r.277 keeps a separate taxable account). Sl.3 (statutory/PPF) and Sl.4
  (recognised funds) each have the limit; it counts "contribution (including aggregate thereof) made
  by that person". Both your accounts are under Sl.4 and in the same EPFO fund, so they add up on
  any reading. Confidence: high.

## 7. AIS and TDS

- EPFO deducts TDS only on the taxable interest it computes under the Rs 2.5 L rule (AF s.393(1)
  Table Sl.5(iii), threshold Rs 10,000; `../ca-questions.md`). It treats the Qualcomm interest as
  exempt: no TDS and no AIS/TIS row in 2024-25, 2025-26 or 2026-27 (to 8 Nov 2026).
- So the returns do not clash with AIS; the risk is only if the department questions it.
