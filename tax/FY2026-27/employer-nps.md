## 2. Rules (verified 2026-11-15)

- 2025 Act s.124(1)(b): private employer, 10% of salary; s.124(2): 14% when taxed under
  s.202(1) (new regime). "Salary" = basic + DA only (s.124(13)(b)).
- s.202(2)(a)(xii): new regime allows no Chapter VIII deduction except s.124(1)-(2) (plus
  s.125(2), s.146). So employer NPS is the one salary deduction kept.
- s.17(1)(h): employer contributions to PF + NPS + superannuation above Rs 7.5 L a year are a
  perquisite; s.17(1)(i): the yearly accretion on that excess is also taxed.
- Exit: Schedule II (s.11) exempts the lump sum on closure up to 60% of the corpus; annuity
  income is taxed at slab (s.124(6)). Partial withdrawal up to 25% of own contributions is
  exempt (Schedule III Sl.4).
- PFRDA (corporate exit FAQ, updated March 2026): normal exit at superannuation, corpus above
  Rs 12 L: up to 80% lump sum, at least 20% annuity. Premature exit: at most 20% lump sum.
  Partial withdrawal after 3 years, 25% of own contributions. The tax text still exempts only
  60%, so the extra 20% would be taxed (checked 2026-11-15: the Act as amended by Finance Act 2026 still says 60%).
- PFRDA corporate page: Active choice up to 75% equity; Auto (Life Cycle) funds taper equity
  from age 35. Contributions go to your own PRAN at once (no vesting) and move with you.

## 3. Tax saving

Marginal rate: 30% x 1.15 surcharge x 1.04 cess = 35.88%. Below Rs 1 Cr it is 34.32% (10%
surcharge). Between Rs 1 Cr and about Rs 1.02 Cr marginal relief applies (computed: tax at
Rs 1 Cr is Rs 28.38 L; relief ends at Rs 1,01,96,947), where each rupee saved is worth more.

## 4. Locked money vs mutual fund (simple)

Rs 100 of pay: NPS gets Rs 100; taking it as salary leaves Rs 64.12 to invest. Same growth
(10x by 60, about 27 years away), exit 60% tax-free, 40% taxed at 35.88% (worst case):
NPS about Rs 856; equity MF after 14.95% LTCG about Rs 555. NPS stays ahead unless it grows
under 6.5x while the MF grows 10x. The cost is liquidity: locked to 60.

## 5. VPF (employee PF)

- New regime: no deduction for EPF/VPF contributions (2025 Act s.123, old regime only).
- Interest on employee contributions above Rs 2.5 L a year (Rs 5 L if no employer PF) is
  taxed at slab (1961 s.10(12) provisos and Rule 9D; 2025 Act Schedule II Sl.4 condition;
  Rules 2026 r.277 per investment-types.md, not re-read). Interest within the limit stays
  tax-free. Employer PF counts toward the Rs 7.5 L cap; employee PF/VPF does not.
- No VPF now (payslip: employee PF = employer PF). Employee PF: Rs 2,82,096 FY2025-26 (EPF
  passbook, printed 2026-08-19), Rs 3,34,816 FY2026-27 (tax computation), Rs 3,70,044 from
  FY2027-28. The Rs 2.5 L limit is already passed, so any VPF earns only taxable interest.
- EPF rate: passbook interest Rs 24,676 for FY2025-26 fits about 8.25%; EPFO site not
  reachable, rate not confirmed. After 35.88% tax: about 5.3%, on money already taxed.
- VPF loses to employer NPS (pre-tax money, tax-deferred growth). Keep VPF at zero; use it
  only if you want a fixed, government-backed debt part.
