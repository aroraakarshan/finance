## 1. The REGAL result (8-K 17 Nov 2026 [S1])
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Patients | 126, randomized 1:1, open-label (not blinded), about 95 sites; AML in CR2 or later (or CRp2) after salvage, not able to get a stem-cell transplant |
| Control arm | Investigator's "best available treatment": observation, hypomethylating drug, venetoclax and/or low-dose cytarabine |
| Primary endpoint | Overall survival (OS) - met |
| Result | Median OS 24.0 vs 14.7 months; hazard ratio 0.59 (41% lower risk of death); two-sided p=0.035 |
| Which analysis | Final analysis at the pre-set 80 deaths (80th reached 28 Oct 2026). The 60-death interim (Jan 2025) did not stop early, so some alpha was spent; the final boundary is not disclosed (not verified) |
| 95% CI (my arithmetic from HR and p) | about 0.36-0.96: the upper end is close to "no effect" |
| Safety | Mostly grade 1-2 injection-site reactions; few patients went to transplant, evenly split |
| Not yet shown | All secondary endpoints (leukemia-free survival, MRD, immune response); full data "at an upcoming major medical conference" (no date) |

Reading it: OS is a hard endpoint, which helps an open-label trial. But it is one small trial with a wide CI. For scale,
oral azacitidine (Onureg) won approval in first remission with 472 patients, double-blind vs placebo, OS 24.7 vs 14.8
months, P<0.001 [S6]. A REGAL control arm at 14.7 months in second remission is high and needs the full data to judge.

## 2. Regulatory path and dates
- Company: meet the FDA "to discuss an accelerated filing strategy", BLA submission in 1H 2027 [S1][S2].
- Designations: Orphan Drug and Fast Track in AML (Fast Track allows a rolling BLA and makes priority review likely
  but not certain); Rare Pediatric Disease for pediatric AML (Oct 2024) [S2].
- PDUFA clock: decision 8 months after submission if priority, 12 months if standard [S9 via post-mortem]. BLA in
  Q2 2027 -> FDA acceptance (day 60) about Q3 2027 -> decision about Q4 2027-Q1 2028 (priority) or Q2 2028 (standard).
  An advisory committee is plausible for a single open-label trial (my judgement).
- Base rate: oncology filing -> approval 92% (BIO 2011-2020) [S10], but that average is for filings the FDA accepted;
  the main risk here comes before that: whether one 126-patient trial with p=0.035 is "substantial evidence".
- Exclusivity: MSK-licensed US composition patents to 2034-2035, own 7-peptide patents to 2040 if granted;
  12-year biologic exclusivity from approval if licensed as a biologic [S3].

## 3. Market size and competition
- US AML: about 20,050 new cases a year; about 7,500 get any second-line treatment [S3]. My estimate: about 25-30%
  reach CR2 and about half of those cannot get a transplant -> about 1,000-1,500 US patients a year (not verified).
  Enrollment speed agrees it is small: 126 patients took about 3 years at 95 sites [S2].
- My US peak sales: low $150 M, base $300 M, high $800 M (needs earlier lines or other WT1 cancers); price assumed
  about $250-300 k a year, similar to oral AML drugs (not verified).
- Competition: Onureg (CR1 maintenance, BMS; sales not broken out, inside "other" [S7]); venetoclax + HMA (frontline
  and salvage, also used as maintenance off-label); menin inhibitors Revuforj (Syndax; $60.7 M sales in Q3 2026,
  $164.4 M in 9 months [S8]; studied as post-transplant maintenance) and Komzifti (Kura; approved 13 Nov 2025; $28.8 M
  in 9 months [S11]); FLT3 and IDH inhibitors. Menin inhibitors bring more patients to CR2 (helps GPS) but may move
  into maintenance themselves. Company: no direct WT1 competitor in CR2 maintenance [S3].
