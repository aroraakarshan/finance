# 7203 Toyota Motor (TSE; ADR TM on NYSE) - stock deep dive

Written 2026-11-17 by `finance-research` (stage 3, global top 30). Research only, not advice; no orders placed.

## Summary
- **BUY NOW Rs 2.02 L: 1 lot of 100 shares at or below JPY 3,500 (about Rs 2.06 L). Add a second lot (Rs 1.8 L) only at JPY 3,050 or lower, and only as a swap, because the 5% idea budget is nearly used. It stays on the buy list.**
- The world's largest carmaker; 56% of its Toyota/Lexus sales are hybrids or EVs. Looking only at the car business (Toyota reports "non-financial services" separately), it has net cash of about JPY 14.6 trn (3.3x EBITDA) and converted 0.84x its profit to FCF over 3 years. The screen's 3.5x net debt and -0.16x FCF come from the car-loan bank, so the screen rule misfired (section 5).
- Expected yearly return over 1-3 years, including dividends: -11.9% / +4.7% / +12.1% (low / base / high), the same in JPY and INR if the yen holds against the rupee (section 9). DCF of the car business: JPY 3,430-5,610 a share, base 4,650.
- Main risk: the US tariff (about JPY 1.1 trn this year) and the yen. A P/E of 10.0 is near the top of its 9-year range (6.3-10.5) after the stock jumped 17% on 5 Nov.

FY Apr-Mar; "FY3/27" = year to 31 Mar 2027 (Toyota: "FY2027"). Price JPY 3,428 = close 16 Nov 2026 (Yahoo `info`, same on stockanalysis [S7][S8]).

## 2. Business
- Builds about 9.7 m cars a year itself (consolidated sales; FY3/27 guide). Counting Daihatsu and retail sales, the group sells 11.0 m [S1].
  H1 FY3/27 revenue by customer location: North America 44%, Japan 14%, Europe 13%, Asia 14%, other 14% [S1].
- Segments, H1 FY3/27: Automotive revenue JPY 23.98 trn, operating income (OI) 1,598 bn (6.7% margin). Financial services (Toyota
  Financial Services, the car loans and leases) revenue 2.76 trn, OI 472 bn. Other 129 bn [S1].
- **Hybrids:** H1 electrified share 55.6% (HEV 2.45 m, +8%; BEV 231 k, +127%; PHEV 130 k). FY guide: 5.0 m HEVs. US hybrid
  inventory is under 5 days. The bottlenecks are hybrids and body-on-frame cars (Land Cruiser) [S2][S3].
  The plan to 2030: about 10 m cars a year, mostly hybrids, with BEVs added on top as demand grows [S2].
- "Value chain" earnings (parts, used cars, finance, connected services): about JPY 2 trn of the 3.9 trn OI; aim +150 bn a year [S2][S3]. Moat: scale, cost (Toyota Production System), hybrid technology and a brand known for reliability. The weak spot is China, where
  Toyota/Lexus H1 retail sales fell 25% to 685 k and BYD and other Chinese makers lead the EV market [S2].

Source: FY2026/FY2025 summaries, "Statements on Non-Financial Services Businesses" [S4][S5]. **3-yr FCF/NI = 0.84** (screen: -0.16).
- Debt of the car business on 31 Mar 2026: JPY 2,800 bn. Liquid assets (cash, deposits and bonds outside finance): 17,418 bn.
  **Net cash 14.6 trn = 3.3x EBITDA** (OI 2,924 + D&A 1,472) [S4]. Screen: net debt 3.5x.
  Liquid assets on 30 Sep 2026: 15,172 bn, after buying back JPY 3.66 trn of shares from Toyota Industries [S1]. Car-business debt is
  not split out for September. Toyota keeps about JPY 7 trn as an emergency reserve [S2].
- The finance arm's debt (40.9 trn) funds loans to car buyers (39.6 trn of receivables) and leased vehicles. Its FY3/26 equity was 6.6 trn
  and its net income 618 bn (ROE about 9%) [S4]. That is why the consolidated numbers look like a highly indebted company with negative FCF.
- Verdict on the screen: misfire confirmed. Measured on the car business alone, Toyota passes both quality drops by a wide margin.
- Shareholder returns: dividend JPY 100 for FY3/27 (95 last year; policy is "stable and continuous" increases). Buybacks:
  3.66 trn (Toyota Industries tender: 1.19 bn shares at JPY 3,067, settled 25 May 2026) plus up to 1.3 trn this year,
  running to 4 Aug 2027. 1.2 bn treasury shares will be cancelled. The treasury-share target falls from 20% to 15% of issued shares.
  The 5-yr plan to FY3/31 returns 11-14 trn to shareholders and spends 22 trn on capex and R&D [S1][S2][S3][S4].

## 7. Management, governance, catalysts
- President Kenta Kon (named on the 5 Nov 2026 filing), CFO Yoichi Miyazaki, Chairman Akio Toyoda [S1][S2].
- **Toyota Industries take-private:** the tender by Toyota Fudosan succeeded on 24 Mar 2026 after the price was raised twice (Jan +15%;
  Mar, to about $38 bn) against opposition from Elliott. Toyota bought back its own shares from Toyota Industries and sold its stake.
  Headline: "Toyota founding family is biggest winner" (Detroit News, 3 Mar 2026). Treat this as a governance flag [S1][S4][S10].
- **Cross-shareholding unwind:** banks and insurers plan to sell about $19 bn of Toyota shares (Reuters, 26 Feb 2026), and the Toyota
  group sold shares in dozens of companies (Nikkei Asia, 12 Jul 2026) [S10]. Toyota absorbs the selling with buybacks: the extra
  JPY 300 bn was "in response to shareholders' requests to sell" [S2]. This is an overhang, but a buyer is in place.
- Past problems: the 2024-25 certification (test-data) scandal [S4]. Toyota is supply-led and keeps buyers waiting [S3].
- **Dated catalysts:** BoJ meeting 18 Dec 2026 (rate hike expected; yen) (`research/events/2026-11-15.md`). Q3 results about
  4 Feb 2027 (Yahoo estimate; the company date is not verified). Year-end dividend JPY 50 (record date 31 Mar 2027, the usual March year-end date; not confirmed). FY3/27 results
  and the FY3/28 guide in early-mid May 2027 (last year's came on 8 May). Buyback window ends 4 Aug 2027.
  Toyota Robotics is set up in Jan 2027; Lexus Shanghai opens in autumn 2027 [S2].

## 8. Risks (what proves the thesis wrong)
- **US tariffs:** cost JPY 1.38 trn in FY3/26 and about 1.1 trn in FY3/27. Some cost will continue ("refunds of parts tariffs" help) [S3][S4].
  North America OI went from -192 bn (FY3/26) to +166 bn (H1) [S1][S2]. A threat of US tariffs on cars built in Canada (Toyota and
  Honda are the most exposed) came up in Reuters on 30 Aug 2026; the article was not read [S10].
- **Yen:** the guide assumes 157/USD. Each 1 yen move vs USD changes OI by roughly JPY 45-50 bn (own estimate from the
  FX bridge [S2]). The yen at 135 would cut OI by about 1 trn. For an INR holder this is partly offset, because a stronger yen raises the rupee value.
- Materials (memory chips, copper, steel) keep rising in FY3/28 [S3]. Further losses in China [S2]. Car-loan credit losses in the
  finance arm. A group deal that favours the family over minority shareholders.
- **Thesis broken if:** the FY3/28 guide (May 2027) puts OI below JPY 3.0 trn; or buybacks stop while banks are still selling;
  or the hybrid lead fades (US inventory days back above 30).
