- What it is: India's commodity exchange (about 98.5% of commodity futures by value [N1]); earns a fee on every gold, silver, crude and gas contract traded, most of it from options.
- Why wait: profit doubled on record gold/silver/crude swings; at P/E 53 TTM (10-yr median 43) and P/B 27 (top 6% of 10 yrs) the price assumes this stays. DCF tested: Rs 1,300-4,000; the price needs about 30% revenue growth fading over 10 years.

## 1. Business
- An exchange plus its own clearing house (MCXCCL). Revenue = transaction fees (futures by value, options by premium), plus
  data, membership and colocation. Costs are mostly fixed (staff Rs 56 Cr, other Rs 133 Cr in Q2), so extra volume is
  nearly all profit: EBITDA margin 76% in Q2 FY27 [S1][N2].
- Q2 FY27 (Jul-Sep): futures ADT Rs 62,819 Cr (+5% q/q), options notional ADT Rs 12 L Cr (+21% q/q), total Rs 12.63 L Cr [N2].
  Options are now over 98% of turnover [N3]. Gold and silver = 66% of futures turnover in H1 FY27 [N4]; crude and
  bullion = 58% of options premium (HDFC Securities) [N2]. Traded clients 19.8 lakh in H1 vs 20.9 lakh in all of FY26 [C1].
- Newer lines (small so far): electricity futures Rs 77 Cr ADT in Q2, about 75% share [C1]; coal exchange subsidiary
  approved by SEBI Apr 2026 (Rs 100 Cr capital) [N5]; far-dated base metal (12 m) and crude (18 m) futures [C1].
- Ownership: no promoter; DIIs 55.2%, FIIs 27.1%, public 17.6% (Sep 2026) [S1].

## 3. Options, SEBI rules and the retail shift
- Retail moved from equity index options to commodity options: equity options STT up 50% to 0.15% this FY vs 0.05% CTT on
  commodity options; SEBI's one weekly expiry rule (Nov 2024), tighter leverage (Jul 2026) and the closing auction session
  (CAS, Aug 2026) made equity F&O costlier [N3]. MCX options premium ADT Rs 10,450 Cr in Q2 (+15% q/q) while NSE/BSE index
  premium fell 27%; retail share of MCX premium 41.6% (Aug 2026, SEBI data) [N3].
- Two-way risk: SEBI rolled back CAS from 7 Dec 2026 [N4], which may pull some traders back to equity; and SEBI could
  apply its retail F&O curbs to commodity options (no proposal found: not verified).
- Tailwind: SEBI board (24 Sep 2026) allowed FPIs into non-agri commodity derivatives incl. physically settled ones (exit 3
  days before expiry) [N6]; circular not yet issued on 4 Nov; 250 FPIs onboarded, now in cash-settled energy only [C1].

## 4. Competition (NSE) and technology
- NSE commodity options premium about Rs 8,300 Cr in Oct 2026 vs Rs 880 Cr in Jan 2026 [N7] (basis not stated - if monthly,
  a few % of MCX's about Rs 2 L Cr a month; not verified). NSE also trades gold receipts (EGR, launched May 2026) and from
  2 Nov 2026 keeps commodities open to 11:55 pm [N8]. MCX CEO: "not much headway" yet; would not guess a share floor [C1].
  An analyst on the call said NSE's retail flow is growing [C1]. NSE listed in 2026 (P/E 47, Rs 5.1 L Cr) [S2].
- Technology: capacity "nearly doubled" in recent months; IT running costs and CME crude licence fees rise with volume [C1].
  A 4-hour outage on 28 Oct 2025 moved trading to the DR site; SEBI was reported to weigh a penalty [N9]. Plan to move the
  primary data centre is "being reviewed" after stakeholder input (colocation concern) [C1][N10, headline only].

## 8. Risks (what proves the thesis wrong)
- Volatility fades: options ADT below Rs 8 L Cr/day for a quarter (Q1 FY27 was Rs 9.9 L Cr [N1]); gold/silver/crude calm.
- NSE gains share: NSE above about 10% of commodity options premium; price war on fees.
- SEBI applies retail F&O curbs or higher margins to commodity options; CTT raised in the Budget.
- Another outage or SEBI action on the data centre/colocation; SGF charge returns (about Rs 35 Cr a quarter, my estimate).
- De-rating: 5-yr max drawdown -41% (2022); -29% in early 2025; volatility about 40% a year [Y].
