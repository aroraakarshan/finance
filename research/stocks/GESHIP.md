## Summary
- **Watch.** India's largest private shipowner (tankers, gas carriers, dry bulk) plus offshore rigs and vessels via Greatship (India) Ltd (GIL) [P]; Rs 1,518, market cap Rs 21,672 Cr [S1].
- Why: trades at 0.68x its own NAV with net cash and a buyback, but profits sit at a war-driven freight peak that the order book can end.
- 3-yr total return (INR): low -32% / base +14% / high +68% (about -12% / +4% / +19% a year; section 9).
- Screen row: rank 1 India, passed G V Q; Rev +47%, EPS +119%, P/E 4.9, EV/EBITDA 3.2 (`scout/ideas.md`, 2026-11-15).

## 2. Business
- Fleet at 2026-11-06 [P]: 42 ships, 3.38 m dwt, average age 14.4 yrs - 5 crude carriers, 16 product
  tankers, 5 gas carriers (LPG/VLGC), 16 dry bulk. GIL: 4 jack-up rigs, 19 offshore support vessels.
- Q2 FY27 segment revenue: shipping Rs 1,845 Cr, offshore Rs 398 Cr; segment result Rs 1,118 Cr vs
  Rs 151 Cr [N1]. So shipping is about 82% of revenue and 88% of segment profit (my arithmetic; Trade
  Brains gives 88.3% for Q1 [N3]).
- Moat: low cost of capital (AAA rating, net cash [P]), long record of buying ships cheap and selling
  high. No pricing power: freight rates are set by world supply and demand. Revenue is USD global
  charter income (region split not verified). Competitors: SCI, Frontline, Scorpio, DHT; offshore SEAMEC.

## 8. Risks
- Freight cycle: war-related rates (Hormuz, sanctions, longer routes) can fall fast if the conflict
  eases; Q2 rates already fell q/q [P]. Order book: crude tankers 28%, product 22%, VLGC 39%, dry bulk 16%
  of fleet [P] - new supply arrives 2027-2029.
- Old fleet (14-17 yrs average); ship values and NAV move with rates.
- Currency: earnings and ship values are in USD; INR weakness helps in INR terms, INR strength hurts.
- Geopolitical: ships trade near war zones (attacks, insurance cost) [P].
- Offshore (GIL): rigs on standby due to the Middle East war [P]; contract repricing 2027-2029.
- Price: 35% yearly volatility, -43% drawdown to Feb 2025 [Y]; 10% below 52-week high.
