## Summary
- **NOT fit for a buy at the 17 Nov open: WATCH.** Do not pay above Rs 270. Buy in steps only on a fall: Rs 1 L at Rs 270 or less, Rs 1 L at Rs 240 or less (Rs 2 L max). Re-check the limit after Q3 FY27 results (about end-Jan/early Feb 2027): raise it only if LME holds above $2,900 and the new refinery is running.
- What it is: a debt-free, government-run (51.28%) low-cost maker of bauxite, alumina and aluminium in Odisha; record profits now (EBITDA margin 47%, ROE 31%) because LME aluminium is near $3,150 after the Gulf war cut supply.
- Why wait: P/E 8.2 looks cheap but is on peak profits: P/B 2.35 is at the 82nd percentile of 10 years, the margin is twice the 10-year median, and the LME futures curve itself prices $2,898 for Dec 2027. The DCF needs a lasting 28% EBIT margin to justify Rs 319 (the 10-year average is near 19%).
- 3-yr total return from Rs 319 (INR, with dividends): low -27% / base +12% / high +50% (about -10% / +4% / +14% a year; section 8). From Rs 265 the base case is about +34% (+10% a year).

## 1. Business (plain words)
- Digs bauxite (Panchpatmali, 6.8 Mt/yr), refines it into alumina (2.1 Mt/yr, 4 streams at Damanjodi), smelts aluminium
  (0.46 Mt/yr at Angul) with its own 1,200 MW coal power plant and its own Utkal coal mine (4.0 Mt FY26, 4.8 Mt target) [PPT][TR].
- Two businesses: (1) alumina, 1,308 kt exported in FY26 by spot tender, at about Rs 24,000/t ($250) cost, among the world's
  lowest [PPT][TR]; (2) aluminium, about 0.47 Mt a year, 95% sold in India at LME plus premium, no hedging [TR].
  Q2 FY27: revenue Rs 5,419 Cr (+26% y/y), PAT Rs 1,835 Cr (+28%); H1 PAT Rs 3,837 Cr (+54%), a record [PPT][TR].
- Sensitivity (my arithmetic, Rs 94/$): each $100/t on LME = about Rs 450 Cr EBITDA (Rs 1.8 EPS); each $50/t on alumina =
  about Rs 680 Cr (Rs 2.8 EPS) today and about Rs 1,060 Cr once the new refinery runs.

## 6. Policy, PSU and governance
- Government holds 51.28% [SC], the floor for PSU status, so a large government share sale is unlikely (last: 2017 [N11]).
  Stock fell 19% on election day 4-Jun-2024 with other PSUs [Y]. CMD Brijendra Pratap Singh; Director (Finance) A K Behuria [TR].
- Mine leases: Panchpatmali central/north end 2029, south 2031; renewal automatic for PSUs but the state may add royalty
  (Odisha already claimed extra royalty in H1 FY27) [TR]. Coal: FSA 6 Mt; rail rake shortages forced costly fuel oil in H1 [TR].
- Holders: FIIs 21.98% (8.99% in Mar-2024), DIIs 11.71% (18.82%) [SC]: crowded foreign money can leave fast. No hedging by
  policy [TR]. Projects run late (5th stream, Pottangi), so the 2030-31 smelter date is a target, not a promise.

## 7. Risks (what proves the WATCH wrong, either way)
- Down: Gulf smelters restart, Hormuz clears -> LME $2,600-2,800 (InCred [N10]); alumina $320-350 as NALCO adds 1 Mt to a
  surplus; input costs, wages, royalty; smelter capex (Rs 25-30k Cr, half the m-cap) lands as LME falls -> dividend cut.
- Up (what would make me buy above Rs 270): LME holds above $3,000 into 2027 (Dec-2027 future is $2,898), alumina above $400
  (Middle East and Indonesian smelters need it), refinery at full rate by mid-2027 and costs fall.
- Price swings: 41% yearly volatility, -48% max fall in 5 years [Y]; 52-week range Rs 250.7-441.5 (high 28-Apr-2026).
