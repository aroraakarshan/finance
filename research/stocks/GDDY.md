## 1. Business (Q3 2026 release, 8-K ex 99.1, 2026-10-29)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Applications & Commerce (A&C) | 524.3 | +9.0% | 46.9% | Website builder (Airo), Microsoft 365 email resale, marketing tools, payments |

| Total | 1,341.8 | +6.0% | NEBITDA 33.5% | International $435.5 m (+3.1%) |

- Domains are the entry product. GoDaddy pays registry fees to Verisign (.com) and others, which it passes on to customers. Renewals make the business sticky. ARR is $4.51 bn (+5.1%) and ARPU is $253 (+6.8%). Customers rose only 75k in 9 months (release), so growth comes from higher prices and more products per customer, not from new customers.
- Airo (AI website and app builder): annualized bookings above $100 m in Q3, up from $50 m in Q2 (release). That is about 2% of revenue. Q3 was the first quarter it "incrementally" added to bookings growth, according to the company. GoDaddy also added APIs so AI agents can buy domains, hosting and email (Developer Platform, release).
- 2026 guidance (release): revenue at the top of $5.215-5.255 bn (+6%); A&C about +10% and Core about +4%; NEBITDA margin above 33%; **FCF about $1.8 bn** (2025: $1.6 bn).

## 3. Margins and returns

- GAAP operating margin rose from 8% (2020) to 23% (2025) and was 27% in Q3 2026: the cost cuts held. A&C earns more (47% segment EBITDA margin) than Core (33%), so the mix keeps lifting margins.
- SBC is $318 m (2025, 6.4% of revenue) and is not deducted from FCF. FCF minus SBC was $1.26 bn in 2025 ($8.95/share).
- ROE has no meaning (equity is negative). ROIC is 21% (scout screen, Yahoo-based; not recomputed). Capex is only 0.5% of revenue. Customers pay upfront, so deferred revenue funds working capital.

## 4. Balance sheet, cash flow, buybacks (negative equity)

- At 2026-09-30 (10-Q / release): cash $1.12 bn, debt $3.84 bn, **net debt $2.72 bn**. That is about 1.6x 2026 NEBITDA (about $1.75 bn = 33.3% x $5.25 bn, own calc) or 1.9x GAAP EBITDA (scout). In Jul 2026 the revolver was raised to $1.2 bn (8-K 2026-08-04). Debt maturities and rates: not verified.
- **Equity -$138.8 m** (2026-09-30; it was +$692 m at end-2024, Yahoo/EDGAR). Buybacks cost $5.4 bn in 2022-2025 (EDGAR) plus $1.4 bn in 2026 to Oct 26 (15.7 m shares, -12% gross diluted shares this year, release). Negative equity here does not mean losses. Buybacks exceed retained profits, so it is a leverage choice. The risk: the company could not buy back if FCF fell, and refinancing at a 5.15% 10-year Treasury rate (FRED 2026-11-13) costs more.
- Shares outstanding: 134.7 m (Dec 2025) -> 122.3 m (Sep 2026). At $112, the $1.8 bn of FCF could retire about 13% of shares a year if all of it went to buybacks.
- No dividend.

## 7. What proves the thesis wrong

- AI agents and chat search cut demand for small-business websites and hosting: 2027 bookings fall below +3%, or the customer count shrinks.
- 2027 FCF target below $1.8 bn, or buybacks slowed by debt or rates.
- Airo is not growing on its own but taking revenue from the old builder ("migration, pricing changes" is in the company's own risk list).
- A bad outcome in the class action (filed Aug 2026: misleading statements on customer growth and bookings; lead-plaintiff deadline 2026-10-20, Kirby/Levi & Korsinsky releases).
- History: -14.3% on 2025-02-14, -14.3% on 2026-02-25 and -16.7% on 2026-07-31 (results days; Reuters 2026-07-30: "narrows annual revenue forecast"). Volatility is 43%/yr over 2 years and the maximum drawdown was -65% (2025-01-28 to 2026-06-22, yfinance).
