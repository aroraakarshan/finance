## 2. Business
- Explosives for coal and metal mines, roads and construction: bulk and packaged explosives, detonators, initiating
  systems. Main customers in India: Coal India and SCCL. Plants in India plus Africa, Turkey, Australia, Indonesia and others; sells to 90+ countries [P1][C1].
- Defence (since about 2010): high-energy explosives, propellants, Pinaka rockets (first private maker), 155 mm
  shells, loitering munitions/drones, counter-drone system Bhargavastra (in trials) [C1].
- Q2 FY27 sales mix [P1]: domestic explosives 27%, international explosives 43%, defence 29%, other 1%.
  Defence share was 22% in H1 FY26, 27% in H1 FY27. So it is still mostly a mining-explosives business.
- Moat: scale and cost leader in a licensed, hazardous industry; the only private firm with a full rocket line.

## 8. Risks (what proves the thesis wrong)
- Defence orders slip: Guided Pinaka (about Rs 3,000 Cr, "before end of FY27") not signed by Mar 2027; H2 FY27 defence below Rs 2,000 Cr [C1].
- Export orders dry up or are blocked (a ceasefire in Europe or the Middle East; government export licences).
- Omnia: overpaying, debt, rand and South Africa risk, a low-margin fertiliser business; or the deal fails after costs.
- Explosive prices: if ammonium nitrate prices fall, sales fall (as in FY24). Coal offtake slows.
- Cash: FY26 free cash flow -Rs 1,072 Cr; another negative year at 30%+ growth would be a warning.
- Price: P/E 75; 37% yearly volatility; -33% fall from Sep 2025 peak to Dec 2025 low (Rs 11,765) [Y]; 52-wk range 11,641-22,700 [S1].
- Safety rules: a blast at another Nagpur explosives maker (SBL Energy, 19 dead, Mar 2026) led to calls for safety
  audits and DPIIT plans for automation [N5]. Solar's own accident record: not checked.
