## 6. AI agents: the core question

- **Threat**: Google's AI Mode now completes hotel bookings with 10 partners in the US (Skift, PhocusWire, blog.google, 2026-08-27; partner list not read); flights to follow. If Google, OpenAI or Meta own the planning chat, OTAs become back-end suppliers paying a toll, like the old search-ad tax but with less brand traffic. Morgan Stanley sees Booking as the better-placed OTA. United now bars AI agents from completing flight bookings (InsideFlyer 2026-11-11): suppliers may also push direct.
- **Defence**: Expedia sells through the agents too: Meta Muse partnership (2026-09-24; stock -8% the next day per TIKR headline), expects "attractive deals" with Meta Muse and Google AI Overviews (Skift 2026-11-04), an app in ChatGPT (since 2025, from memory, not verified this run), and B2B (its API supply and payments are what an agent needs to book). The company calls AI "new sources of growth" (release); its 10-Q lists AI-powered platforms as a new competitive risk.
- No hard data yet on how much traffic or margin agents take. Watch B2C bookings growth (7% now) and B2C marketing as % of bookings (4.5%): if B2C growth falls below about 3% while marketing cost rises, the bear case is under way.

## 8. What proves the thesis wrong

- B2C bookings growth below 3% for two quarters or 2027 bookings guidance below +5%: AI and Booking/Airbnb are taking share.
- B2B growth below 12% (its main engine) or B2B margin falling further.
- Buybacks slowed or debt-funded; deferred merchant bookings falling y/y (float outflow).
- Travel recession: a wider Middle East war or US consumer slowdown (2020 showed revenue can halve).
