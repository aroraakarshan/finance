## Summary
- **Drop.** Single-site refiner (Manali, Chennai, 210,000 bpd), subsidiary of Indian Oil (IOC), sells almost all fuel to IOC [N3][N5]; Rs 1,428.20, market cap Rs 21,268 Cr [S1].
- Why: P/E 3.7 rests on a record GRM of USD 20.41/bbl from the 2026 war; mid-cycle P/E is about 10 and two large capex plans are unpriced.
- 3-yr total return (INR): low -33% / base -5% / high +26% (about -12% / -2% / +8% a year; section 9).
- Screen row: rank 2 India, passed G V; Rev +26%, EPS +212%, P/E 3.7, EV/EBITDA 2.7, ROE 51% (`scout/ideas.md`, 2026-11-15).

## 2. Business
- One product line: crude refining into diesel, petrol, ATF, LPG, lubes, wax and petrochemical feed [N6].
  Revenue is almost all India (sales to IOC); exports and product-wise split not verified.
- Crude throughput Q2 FY27: 2.59 MMT [N2]. Old Cauvery Basin (Nagapattinam) refinery shut in 2019 [N3].
- New: first own retail fuel outlet opened Mar 2026; plan for 300 outlets by mid-2028 [N3][N5].
- Moat: weak. Price taker on crude and product cracks; benefits from IOC's offtake and logistics.
  Competitors: IOC (parent), BPCL, HPCL, MRPL, Reliance, Nayara; Asian export refiners.

## 7. Management and governance
- Promoters 67.29% (unchanged since 2023): Indian Oil 51.89% and Naftiran Inter Trade Co (Iran) 15.40%
  at Sep 2026 [S4]. An Iranian state-linked co-promoter matters during the 2026 Iran war (sanctions,
  board role); effect not verified. MD: H Shankar [N4][N5].
- Capital allocation run by IOC: steady 30-35% payout; the Nagapattinam project was restructured after
  poor return (IRR) on the refinery plan [N4]. Transfer pricing to IOC sets CPCL's realised margins.
- Controversies: local protests over land for the project (families seeking relief, Aug 2026) [N7].

## 8. Risks
- Margin cycle: GRM fell from USD 14.1 to 8.78 in one quarter, then rose to 20.41 [N1]; FY25 GRM collapse
  cut EPS to Rs 14 [S1]. A ceasefire or new Asian capacity can halve profits within a quarter.
- Government: price controls on retail fuel, possible windfall tax on refiners (India had one 2022-2024;
  2026 status not verified), PSU dividend demands.
- Crude supply and inventory losses (Hormuz disruption cuts both ways).
- Capex: two large projects with no stated cost; parent IOC controls the decision.
- Single-asset risk (one refinery; outages hit all earnings).
- Price: 52% yearly volatility, -61% drawdown to Feb 2025 [Y]; up 71% in 2026 to Oct [N1].
