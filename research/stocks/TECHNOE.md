## 1. Business in plain words
- Builds high-voltage substations (132-765 kV) for Power Grid, Adani Energy, DVC, state firms and IndiGrid; claims
  ~350 of India's ~500 substations of 400 kV+ [P p14]. A contractor: it buys transformers from GE Vernova, Hitachi.
- Order book Rs 10,100 Cr (Sep 2026): transmission 65%, smart meters 14%, FGD 7%, distribution 7%, TBCB 4%, data
  centre 3%; Power Grid 37%, Adani Energy 12%, Resonia 8%, DVC 8%, RVPN 7% [P p10].
- Smart meters: owns and runs 2.24 m meters (MP, J&K, Jharkhand, Tripura) for 10 years, paid monthly by state
  distributors; 85% installed [P p15]. Data centres: Chennai 5 MW built of 24 MW, **0.5 MW leased** (same as in
  May); Noida 0.5 MW live; Kolkata 12 MW by FY29; AP 2 MW for an unnamed "global hyperscaler"; 250 MW by FY32 [P p17][C].

- AngelOne review's -0.29 OCF/profit over FY24-26 is exactly right (-335 / 1,165). Standalone (the parent alone) was
  -75 Cr over the same 3 years [SC]: even the EPC arm did not turn profit into cash.
- Why (FY26 cash flow [AR]): receivables -Rs 544 Cr (back-ended billing), contract and other assets -Rs 867 Cr;
  payables +325, advances +103. Contract assets: Rs 249 Cr (Mar-24) -> 709 -> 1,480 (Mar-26) [AR]. Management: about
  Rs 600 Cr is two lines built for IndiGrid (Ishanagar, Dhule), paid at handover; the rest mainly smart meters, whose
  build cost the accounting puts in operating cash flow [C4][AR note 3.2].
- So the negative OCF is mostly the company acting as its own lender, not lost money. But the cash is at risk:
  meter payers are state distributors; contract balances net of advances were 71% of FY26 revenue (Rs 2,306 Cr)
  [AR]. Emphasis of matter: Rs 90 Cr overdue for years (Afghanistan, Bengal Energy arbitration, REC case) [R].
- Test: Ishanagar paid (90% received in Q3 FY27), Dhule (about Rs 450 Cr) due by Mar 2027 [P p14][C]. If FY27 OCF
  is not clearly positive with those two cashed, the bear case wins.
- Net cash about Rs 2,270 Cr (Sep-26: investments 2,058 + cash 266 - debt 55; 20% of market value) [P p8][SC].
- Data-centre funding: Rs 658 Cr spent to Sep 2026 (Chennai 565) [P p18]. The Rs 1,250 Cr QIP (Jul 2024, Rs 1,438 a
  share) put Rs 550 Cr into Chennai, incl. Rs 200 Cr moved from a cancelled grid project [Q]. Plan Rs 1,000 Cr more
  in FY27 [C][C4], but only about Rs 58 Cr went in H1 (658 vs "about 600" in May). 250 MW at Chennai's cost needs
  several thousand crore, more than the cash: funding beyond FY28 not disclosed (debt, partner or new shares).
