## Summary
- **WATCH: Rs 0 now; entry $118 or lower (about Rs 11,100/share, ~37 shares = Rs 4.1 L, the 1% cap) only if Q3 (24 Nov 2026) shows operating margin of 12.5%+ without tariff refunds and Abercrombie-brand growth holding. Otherwise SKIP.**
- What it is: US teen/young-adult clothing seller (Abercrombie, Hollister); 15 quarters of sales growth, no borrowings, buys back 6-8% of its shares a year.
- Why not now: price doubled in 6 months ($70.57 -> $145.77). The screen's P/E 12.5 includes a one-off $1.75/share tariff refund; without it, P/E is 14.7 (own post-2023 median about 11). Margins are 3-5x their 2015-2022 level (peak risk), and new US tariffs hit its Vietnam/Cambodia sourcing.
- 1-3 yr return (to Nov 2028, no dividend): USD low -42% / base +7% / high +44%; INR -38% / +15% / +54% (section 9). DCF $111-182, base $156.

## 1. Business
- Two brand families (Q2 FY2026 sales, 8-K 26 Aug 2026): **Abercrombie** (adults ~25-35, plus Abercrombie Kids) $597m, 47%; **Hollister** (teens, incl. Gilly Hicks) $670m, 53%.
- Regions Q2: Americas 81% (+5%), EMEA 16% (+2%), APAC 3% (+19%). Stores, own website and app; franchises in the Middle East and Asia. Fiscal year ends late Jan (FY2025 = year to 31 Jan 2026).
- Sourcing FY2025 (10-K): Vietnam 37%, Cambodia 26%, India 11%, other 26%; 124 vendors in 15 countries. All imported, so US tariffs land directly on cost of goods.
- China: seeking outside backers for its China business (Bloomberg 4 Aug 2026, headline); Q2 release risk text mentions a "review of strategic alternatives for our APAC region".

## 4. Margins and returns
- Gross margin (sales minus cost of sales excl. D&A, XBRL): Q3 FY25 62.5% (Q3 FY24 65.1%, tariffs); Q1 FY26 62.8%; Q2 FY26 71.1% reported, 63.2% without the $100m refund (Q2 FY25 62.6%).
- Tariff cost: FY2025 about $90m (170 bp of sales, Q3 FY25 release). FY26 assumes 10-12.5% effective US tariff for the rest of the year (Q2 release), i.e. the new Section 301 "forced-labor" duties (10% or 12.5% on 60 countries from 24 Jul 2026, Ward and Smith 27 Jul 2026), replacing the 10% Section 122 surcharge after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs (Feb 2026, NRF).
- ROE FY2025 37% (NI / avg equity, XBRL); Yahoo TTM 41%. ROIC excl. leases about 80% (NOPAT ~$500m on ~$620m equity minus cash); about 28% if $1.17 bn of store leases count as capital. The screen's "ROIC 99%" ignores leases.

## 5. Balance sheet and cash flow
- 1 Aug 2026 (Q2 release): cash $628m + securities $10m; **no borrowings** (senior notes repaid 2024; $500m ABL undrawn). Operating leases $1,168m (31 Jan 2026, 10-K).
- FCF (CFO - capex, XBRL): FY2023 $496m, FY2024 $527m, FY2025 $378m, H1 FY26 $184m. FY26 capex ~$250m (guide, up from $183m in FY24).
- Buybacks: FY2024 $230m, FY2025 $451m (11% of shares), H1 FY26 $286m (7%); FY26 plan at least $500m; $568m authorization left (1 Aug 2026). Buybacks exceed FCF, so cash fell $760m -> $628m in H1. Sustainable pace about FCF (~$400m = 6.5% of market cap a year).
- No dividend since 2020 (yfinance dividend history: last $0.20 on 5 Mar 2020).

## What proves the thesis wrong
- Underlying op. margin below 12% in Q3/Q4 FY26 or FY27 guide below FY26 underlying EPS (~$11).
- Abercrombie comps turn negative again while Hollister stays negative (both brands down = fashion miss).
- Inventory rising well ahead of sales (was -0.2% y/y on 1 Aug 2026 vs +5% sales: fine now).
- New tariffs lifting the Vietnam/Cambodia rate above ~15%, or buybacks cut below FCF.
