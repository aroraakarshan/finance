## Summary
WATCH: buy below CHF 385 in two steps (Rs 2.2 L = 5 shares at or below CHF 385, Rs 1.5 L = 4 shares at or below CHF 340; at most 9 shares, about Rs 3.7 L), and only if the 19 May 2027 results confirm Delivery Systems growth of at least 13% with no guidance cut.
What it is: a Swiss maker of autoinjectors and pens that drug companies fill with their injectable drugs. It now makes nothing else: the insulin-pump business was sold in Jul 2025. Sales grew +17.5% in H1 FY27 with a 35.7% EBIT margin. The weak points: capex peaks this year, so free cash flow is about zero, and at CHF 429 the price is close to its 52-week high, 29x this year's earnings and about equal to the DCF base value.
1-3 yr return from CHF 429: low -33%, base +15%, high +47% in CHF; in INR low -33%, base +25%, high +65% (CHF/INR assumptions in section 9). Bought at CHF 385, the base case is about +40% in INR.
Key risk: pills (Wegovy pill, Foundayo) and cheap generic semaglutide take share from injections, and Ypsomed has to cut its prices for high-volume customers. Next dated catalyst: FY 2026/27 results on 19 May 2027 [Y2].

## 2. Business
- Ypsomed makes "platform" devices (YpsoMate autoinjector, UnoPen and YpsoFlow pens, the large-volume YpsoDose and new YpsoLoop and YpsoDot). Drug companies customise them and file them with the drug. Contracts run about 10 years. Major costs (plastic, energy, labour) are passed on through indexation. Most contracts are in CHF. Customers pay capacity reservation fees, and prices are volume-based (lower per unit at higher volumes) [Y6].
- 280+ programs: 190 in clinical trials and 90+ on the market. A record 30 new projects in H1 FY27 (44 in all of FY26), mostly for autoimmune drugs, a third with new customers. More than 80% of autoinjector drug approvals in developed markets in the last 12 months (outside drug makers' own devices) used YpsoMate (company analysis) [Y1][Y3]. Example: Innovent's mazdutide (an obesity drug) launched in China in a YpsoMate [Y5].
- Plants: Burgdorf and Solothurn (CH), Schwerin (DE; Schwerin II opens mid-2027), Changzhou (CN; production since end-2025), Holly Springs (US; opens end-2027) [Y3]. **Discrepancy:** the FY deck in May said Schwerin II would start its first lines at the end of 2026 [Y6]; the Nov deck says mid-2027, so it slipped about 6 months.
- Sales by invoice address, FY26: Switzerland 107.6 m, rest of Europe 401.5 m, North America 64.6 m, rest of world 157.2 m (customers buy mainly through European subsidiaries) [Y5].

## 8. What proves the thesis wrong
- DS growth below 10% in a half year, or an FY27/28 guidance cut. The order book (280+ programs) would then not be turning into volume.
- Pills: the Wegovy pill has had 8 m prescriptions since Jan 2026, and Lilly's Foundayo was approved in Apr 2026 (sector note [Y14]). Stevanato's GLP-1 revenue is already falling. If pills take most new obesity patients, Ypsomed's 40+ incretin projects are worth less. Its broad base (autoimmune and other biologics) limits but does not remove this risk.
- Generic semaglutide (India Mar 2026, Canada May 2026, China; Brazil approvals Nov 2026 [Y14]) cuts the price per dose. That brings more biosimilar makers as customers (two-thirds of the incretin projects) but puts pressure on device prices.
- EBIT margin below 30%, or ROCE falling well below 20% as the new plants fill slowly.
- More related-party deals at doubtful prices with Michel-family companies.

## 11. Management and governance
- The Michel family holds 71.7% (31 Mar 2026). Founder Willy Michel holds about two-thirds; his son Simon Michel is CEO and a board member [Y5][Y7]. The articles cap any other holder's votes at 5% and raise the mandatory-bid threshold to 49%. Minority holders cannot influence the company.
- Related-party dealings: the Diabetes Care sale to Willy Michel's TecMed (an independent valuation was obtained); the Burgdorf office lease from Techpharma, another Michel company (CHF 0.6 m a year); services contracts; and the CHF 100 m buyback from Willy Michel at the same CHF 300 as public holders [Y5][Y7]. All are disclosed. The terms look fair on paper, but this is the main governance risk.
