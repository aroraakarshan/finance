## 6. Management and governance
- Prem Watsa, 75, Chairman and CEO since 1985; salary $600,000, no bonus or equity since 2000 [S6]. Book per share +18.7% a year since 1985 with dividends [S3].
- Control: his SixtyTwo company holds all 1.548m multiple voting shares (50 votes each, capped at 41.8%); with his own shares **43.3% of the votes** on about 9.6% of the shares (own calc) [S6]. A takeover needs him.
- Succession: no named successor; the proxy describes only a yearly board review [S6]. Son Ben Watsa, 47, is a director and Fairfax India chairman [S6][S3]. CFO changed Mar 2025 (Amy Sherk) [S4]. Key-man risk is higher than at Berkshire, where Greg Abel is CEO since 1 Jan 2026.
- Feb 2024: short seller Muddy Waters alleged accounting manipulation (incl. Digit, related-party deals); Fairfax rejected it (news headlines 9-16 Feb 2024 [S7], report not read).
- Style: big concentrated bets (Eurobank, India, now IDBI and Boots). Book grew only 3.8% a year in 2011-15 and 5.6% in 2016-20; Watsa names BlackBerry as a mistake [S3].

## 7. Risks (what proves the thesis wrong)
- Soft insurance market: combined ratio above 97% for a full year, or book growth under 8% a year for 2 years.
- IDBI: a $5.6bn state-bank turnaround; unions strike on 3 Dec 2026 and ask RBI to block it [S7]; RBI may require a fix for owning both IDBI and CSB Bank (headline, not verified). Debt to capital above about 32% would be a warning.
- Concentration: Eurobank (Greece) and India together could be close to half of book plus hidden value after IDBI (own estimate).
- Watsa health or a messy succession; dual-class control.
- P/B falls back to its 1.06x 10-yr median: -26% with no change in the business. Max drawdown 61% in USD (to May 2020) [S5].
