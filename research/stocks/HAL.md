## Summary
- **NOT fit for a buy at the 17 Nov open (no buy above Rs 4,350). WATCH, then BUY IN STEPS: Rs 1.5 L at or below Rs 4,350 and Rs 1.5 L at or below Rs 3,900 (max Rs 3 L). Or buy the first Rs 1.5 L at up to Rs 4,800 once the first Tejas Mk1A is handed over to the IAF (reported for Jan 2027).**
- What it is: India's state-owned maker of fighter jets, helicopters and their engines; almost all sales to the Ministry of Defence. Debt-free, order book about Rs 2.5 lakh crore (about 7x yearly sales) [D1].
- Why wait: the P/E of 33 looks cheap for Indian defence, but about a third of profit is interest on customer advances; on the core business the P/E is about 42. Tejas Mk1A is about 3 years late, HAL lost the AMCA stealth-jet prototype race, and margins fall as more of the work is manufacturing.
- 3-yr total return from Rs 4,789 (INR): low -18% / base +25% / high +72% (about -6% / +8% / +20% a year); from Rs 4,350 the base is +38% (+11%/yr).

## 1. Business
- Builds Su-30MKI (Russian licence), Tejas, HTT-40 trainers, Dornier 228, Dhruv, Prachand and LUH helicopters; makes
  AL-31FP and Shakti engines; repairs the IAF fleet [S1]. Government owns 71.64% [S1]. Repair work earns more, so
  margins drop as deliveries ramp up (Motilal: EBITDA margin 29.4% FY27 -> 27.2% FY29) [D1]; split not in sources read.
- Moat: India's only maker of military aircraft; weakness: one customer sets prices, and suppliers abroad (GE engines,
  Israeli radar) set the pace of delivery.

## 3. Margins, returns, cash
- ROE 24% (FY26), 3-yr 26%; ROCE 32%; no debt; dividend yield 0.94%, payout about 32% [S1].
- Cash Rs 46,196 Cr (Mar 2026) [Y], funded by customer advances (other liabilities Rs 1,01,875 Cr, Sep 2026) [S1]; its interest
  (other income Rs 4,087 Cr TTM) is 32% of pre-tax profit. Ex-cash P/E (3,19,605 - 46,196) / (9,588 - 75% x 4,087) = 41.9.
- Working capital is getting heavier: inventory days 544 (FY24) -> 859 (FY26), working-capital days 37 -> 301 [S1];
  inventory +39% y/y in Q2 (Citi: "execution preparedness") [N2]. Operating cash flow Rs 13,643 Cr FY25, Rs 10,906 Cr
  FY26; free cash flow FY22-26 Rs 42,534 Cr vs profit Rs 36,009 Cr [S1] - good, because of advances.

## 8. OFS risk (government stake sales)
- Government stake 89.97% (IPO Mar 2018) -> 75.15% (Mar 2021) -> 71.64% (Mar 2023), unchanged since [S1]; down to 51% = 20.6% more, about Rs 66,000 Cr.
- 2026 is a record OFS year: Rs 59,229 Cr by Aug from 10 PSUs incl. Coal India, LIC and Cochin Shipyard (5%, Jul) [N15];
  DIPAM (24 Oct): "target reached, but asset monetisation to continue" [N16]. No HAL OFS announced. An OFS is priced at a
  discount and can knock the price for days (not verified for HAL): an entry chance more than a thesis risk.

## 9. Risks (what proves the thesis wrong)
- Fewer than 6 Tejas Mk1A delivered by 31 Mar 2027, or FY27 sales growth under 10% (guidance 10-12%).
- EBITDA margin below 23% for two quarters outside Q4 (mix shift worse than the 27% FY29 brokers expect).
- No major new contract (helicopters, Su-30 upgrade) signed by end-2027 while the book is consumed.
- Private firms (Tata, L&T, Bharat Forge) win more aircraft work after AMCA; IAF buys foreign jets (MRFA) instead.
- Lower rates or smaller advances (a third of profit is interest); de-rating: max fall 5 yrs -44.5% (to Feb 2025),
  volatility 28.6% a year [Y]; OFS supply.
