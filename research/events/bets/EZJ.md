# EZJ - easyJet plc (LSE, GBp), Apollo (Eagle Bidco) cash scheme at 715p

Checked 2026-11-18 by finance-research. Prices are 17 Nov 2026 closes (Yahoo, yfinance TZ=UTC); GBP/INR 123.43 (Yahoo GBPINR=X, 17 Nov).

## Summary
- VERDICT: NO-GO at 689p. RESIZE the scout's trigger: buy Rs 1 L only at 660p or less (not 670p), and only if the dip comes with no bad regulatory news.
- After the 0.5% stamp duty and 35.88% tax, 689p pays 2.1% (5.3% a year if paid 14 Apr 2027); a break costs about 43%. It needs a 95% chance to break even; the market prices 92%, the base rate is about 90%.
- At 670p it needs 91% (10.1% a year after tax): still break-even at best. At 660p it needs 89% (12.9% a year).
- Main risk: no precedent for a private-equity buyer of an EU airline; the EU said in Jul 2026 it will review airline ownership rules (Reuters).

## The event (primary sources)
- Offer: 715p cash per share (Rule 2.7 announcement, 6 Aug 2026). 81% over the 394p close on 28 May 2026, the Unaffected Date (2.7, premium list).
- Votes passed 11 Nov 2026: Court Meeting 97.07% of scheme shares voted FOR; General Meeting 96.67% (RNS "Result of Court Meeting and General Meeting", 11 Nov 2026, Investegate 9870224). Conditions 2(a) and 2(b) met.
- Court sanction hearing: no date yet. Timetable: hearing on day "D", set after Bidco confirms all other conditions, "expected before the end of the first calendar quarter of 2027"; last dealing D+1, effective D+2, cash within 14 days of the effective date (RNS 11 Nov, Appendix).
- Long-Stop Date: 11.59 p.m. 6 Aug 2027, extendable by agreement (with Panel consent) (RNS 11 Nov; 2.7 definitions).
- Dividend: the offer assumes none. Any dividend before the effective date lets Bidco cut the price by the same amount (2.7, para 3), so it does not change the total. FY2026 results expected 25 Nov 2026 (Yahoo calendar; date not confirmed by easyJet).

## Conditions still open (2.7, Appendix I, Part A, cond. 3)
- Aviation licences (3a): Swiss FOCA (easyJet Switzerland), Austro Control / Austrian ministry (easyJet Europe), UK CAA (easyJet UK operating and route licences, ATOL licences): any approval they say is needed, and no licence suspended or threatened at closing.
- Merger control: Austria (3b), Egypt (3c), Germany FCO (3d), UK CMA (3e: no questions after a briefing paper, or Phase 1 clearance). No European Commission merger condition is listed.
- Foreign investment: Austria (3f), France (3g), Italy (3h), Malta (3i), Spain (3j), UK NSI Act (3k).
- Court sanction (2c). Status: the scheme document (15 Oct) said some approvals were obtained in Austria, Britain, France, Germany and Malta, others outstanding (Reuters via RTE, 15 Oct 2026). Which ones exactly: NOT verified (scheme document not reached). No RNS on clearances found to 17 Nov (Investegate company list).
- Bidco must take "all necessary steps" for merger control and "best endeavours" for other regulatory conditions, limited to the easyJet group (2.7, Co-operation Agreement).

## Ownership and control: how the structure tries to pass (2.7 para 2, 4, 5, App. IV)
- Rules: EU Reg. 1008/2008 (easyJet Europe, Austria), UK Civil Aviation Act plus assimilated 1008/2008 (easyJet UK), Swiss law, the UK-EU TCA and the UK-Swiss air services agreement (2.7 definition). Majority EU ownership AND effective control.
- Topco after closing: Apollo Funds at most 49.9%; rollover holders 45.1-49.9% (the Haji-Ioannou family elects rollover for 116,061,871 shares); an "EU Trust" (management plan) up to 5% in B shares.
- Most of Apollo's money goes in as GBP 750 m - 1 bn of Midco 1 preference shares (14% fixed dividend) below Topco, so Apollo's ordinary stake stays under 50%.
- Topco board of 9: Apollo names the chair and 3 directors, but a majority must be EU Nationals; chair has no casting vote; deadlocks go to A and B holders by more than 50% (RNS 11 Nov clarification).
- Articles allow weighted voting (raise EU votes, cut non-EU votes), removing non-EU directors, and forced sale or buy-back of non-EU rollover shares (not of Apollo's).
- Open questions: whether the family's holding vehicles count as EU-controlled, and how the UK licence test (UK vs EU nationals after Brexit) is met: NOT verified. easyJet itself is only 35.07% EU-owned today; it suspended votes on 233,921,894 non-EU shares at the GM (RNS 11 Nov).
- EU review of ownership rules "likely in the autumn" to stop foreign investors gaining effective control (EU official, Reuters via RTE, 22 Jul 2026). Outcome: not found. European Works Council opposes the deal (scheme document, via Reuters/RTE 15 Oct).
