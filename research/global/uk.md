# United Kingdom - cheap-ish, low-tech, high-dividend; mind the 0.5% stamp duty

Written 2026-11-15. Market research, not advice; the user decides. Sources [K#] at the end.
User context: 81% of Rs 2.23 Cr in QCOM + MSFT (US tech). The UK is almost the opposite:
banks, oil, pharma, staples, mining; very little tech.

## Summary
- The UK fills his gaps (banks, energy, pharma, staples; almost no tech) and pays no dividend withholding.
- But P/E 16.9 is well above its 10y (13.5) and 20y (12.5) averages; 1y USD return 15.6%.
- Verdict: neutral. Hold it through a pan-Europe ETF (about 22% UK); add CSUKX (acc, 0.07%) only for a value tilt.
- Single UK shares cost 0.5% stamp duty per buy (STT from late 2027, same rate); Irish ETFs do not.

## 1. Access for an Indian resident

- IBKR: LSE yes (UK listed on IBKR Europe commission page [K1]; Fixed 0.05%, min GBP 3, see
  [../themes/ibkr-global/equities.md](../themes/ibkr-global/equities.md)). LRS: listed shares/ETFs allowed; no
  margin, CFDs or options abroad.
- **Stamp duty**: buying shares of a UK company (or a foreign company with a UK register)
  costs 0.5% SDRT; 1.5% when shares go into depositary receipt / clearance schemes; no
  stamp duty on foreign shares bought outside the UK [K2]. Late 2027 it becomes the
  "Securities Transfer Tax", same 0.5% rate (Budget 28 Oct 2026 [K3]); draft rules put
  shares of non-UK-incorporated companies outside it [K4]. New UK listings: 3-year stamp
  duty holiday from 27 Nov 2025 [K3].
- Irish UCITS ETFs are Irish companies, so the GOV.UK charge list does not catch them [K2];
  no HMRC line saying "Irish ETFs are exempt" was found (not verified directly). Practical
  rule: 0.5% on every buy of HSBC/Shell/AZN on LSE; nothing on ISF/VUKE.

## 7. Risks

- Energy-driven inflation and more rate hikes (BoE [K15]); OBR cut fiscal headroom to
  GBP 15 bn after the Middle East shock [K3].
- Concentration: top 10 = 49% of FTSE 100; HSBC alone 10% [K5].
- Global earners: FTSE 100 revenue is mostly earned abroad (share not verified), so it is
  less of a "UK economy" bet than it looks.
- Stamp duty makes frequent trading of single UK shares costly (0.5% per buy).
- No capital controls.

## 8. Dividend withholding and India DTAA

UK withholds no tax on ordinary dividends (PwC UK, reviewed 2026-07-23, lists WHT only for
interest and royalties [K16]); so nothing to reclaim and no FTC. Taxed in India at slab.

Tax one-liners (from tax/investment-types.md): foreign shares/ETFs LTCG 12.5% after 24 months,
else slab; dividends at slab; Irish UCITS accumulating: no tax until sale; US-situs > $60k:
US estate tax.
