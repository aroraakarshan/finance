## Summary

- Angel funds (SEBI Cat I AIF) take new money only from accredited investors; older funds stop non-accredited money on 2027-03-31 [N1, N2]. He is not accredited, so this is closed to him.
- Unlisted/pre-IPO platforms are not recognised by SEBI [N5]; NSE, HDB Financial, Tata Capital and others had IPO prices 13-71% below the last unlisted price [N10, N12].
- What to do: buy neither now; if ever accredited, at most 2-5% of the portfolio via a SEBI angel fund [N3].

## 2. Venture and angel investing in India

- Rules: angel funds are SEBI Category I AIFs. Funds registered after 2025-09-10 may take money
  only from accredited investors; older funds may keep up to 200 non-accredited investors until
  2027-03-31, then take no new money from them (SEBI circular of 2026-09-07/08) [N1, N2].
- Accreditation (SEBI AIF Reg 2(1)(ab)): income >= Rs 2 Cr, or net worth >= Rs 7.5 Cr with
  Rs 3.75 Cr financial, or income Rs 1 Cr plus net worth Rs 5 Cr [N1]. He meets none on the
  figures we have (net worth Rs 2.23 Cr; his income is below Rs 1 Cr: nv). So: closed to him.
- **Discrepancy:** Livemint (2026-09-08) says the minimum per investor is Rs 25 L (was Rs 50 L),
  max Rs 5 Cr, and an angel fund puts Rs 10 L-25 Cr into each startup with a 6-month lock-in on
  third-party exits [N2]. ET (2026-06-08) says the 2025 rules "removed ... minimum corpus and
  investment threshold" [N3]. SEBI's 2025 amendment text not read (nv).
- Other AIFs (Cat I VC funds): Rs 1 Cr minimum; Rs 10 L for some with accreditation
  ([india-beyond-mf/global-and-private.md](../../../themes/india-beyond-mf/global-and-private.md) section 15).
- Platforms: LetsVenture runs "LV Angel Fund", a SEBI-registered Cat I angel fund; it shows 36k+
  platform investors, Rs 1,160+ Cr committed, 715+ deals (site, read 2026-11-15) [N4]. IPV
  (Inflection Point Ventures) launched a USD 110 m fund in GIFT City (Business Standard,
  2025-07-31; headline only, page 403) [N6]. AngelList India site did not load (nv).
- Expert sizing (ET, 2026-06-08) [N3]: Rs 30-40 L is too small; a spread portfolio needs
  Rs 1-2 Cr; LetsVenture's founder suggests 20+ companies over 5 years at Rs 10 L+ a year; cap at
  2-5% of the portfolio; expect 7-10 years before money comes back.
- Return evidence: most startups fail and a few winners make all the money (power law). One
  investor with 25+ startups had three fail completely (ET, 2026-06-08) [N3]. The academic study
  usually cited, Wiltbank and Boeker, "Returns to Angel Investors in Groups" (Kauffman, 2007)
  exists (Crossref metadata) [N8], but its figures could not be read (SSRN blocked): nv.
- Funding climate (Tracxn): 2025 funding $10.5 bn, down about 17%; rounds down 39% to 1,518;
  seed down 30% to $1.1 bn (TechCrunch, 2025-12-27) [N9]. FY26 $11.7 bn, down 18% (ET headline,
  2026-04-08); H1 2026 up 12% with "bigger cheques to fewer startups" (Fortune India headline,
  2026-06-25) [N9]. So the winter has eased but seed money is still scarce.
- Fees: platform carry and setup fees: nv (not on public pages).
- Tax: Cat I AIF is pass-through; unlisted shares 24 m / 12.5% LTCG, else slab
  ([tax/investment-types.md](../../../../tax/investment-types.md)).


## 3. Pre-IPO and unlisted shares

- Regulator view: SEBI press releases of 2024-12-09 and 2026-06-17 say platforms that let people
  trade shares of unlisted public companies are "neither authorized nor recognized by SEBI"; buyers
  get no exchange grievance redress or SEBI ODR [N5]. Unlisted Zone itself says it is "not a
  SEBI-recognised stock exchange" and prices are "indicative" (site, 2026-11-15) [N7]. InCred
  Unlisted says it is not a SEBI-registered adviser [N7]. **UNREGULATED.**
- SEBI (2026-07-31): an off-market sale by an existing holder to up to 200 buyers is not a public
  issue [N11]. This makes the deals legal private transfers, not regulated trades.
