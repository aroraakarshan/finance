# Collectibles: art, watches, wine, coins, cars, fractional art

Part of [Gold, insurance, deposits and collectibles](../gold-insurance-deposits-collectibles.md). Written 2026-11-15. Source codes [Sx]: [sources](sources.md). "nv" = not verified. Fit 1 (skip) to 5 (strong fit).

## Summary

- All unregulated, 20%+ round-trip costs, poor liquidity: fit 1 for every item.
- Art returns fall from 10% to 6.5%/yr once selection bias is removed; watch prices are ~29% below their 2022 peak.
- Fractional art platforms abroad are likely not allowed under LRS (unlisted foreign equity = ODI).

- **Indian art market now.** Saffronart autumn 2026: all 200 lots sold, Rs 250.58 Cr (live Rs 218.62 Cr,
  online Rs 31.97 Cr) [S39]; Raja Ravi Varma record Rs 167 Cr (BusinessLine headline, 2026-04-02 [S40]).
  Records are at the top end; [S29] finds the top end underperforms.
- **Antiquities.** Objects over 100 years old are antiquities; ~3.5 L are privately registered with ASI
  (Deccan Herald 2022-06-13 [S41]). Export is restricted to the government under the 1972 Act
  (Wikipedia summary [S42]; Act text not fetched). So such pieces cannot be sold abroad: a smaller market.
- **Fractional art.** No SEBI framework for fractional art (SEBI's small-and-medium REIT licence covers
  real estate only, HT 2024-08-09 [S43]). Foreign platforms sell unlisted shares; for a resident that is
  unlisted foreign equity, i.e. ODI, not portfolio investment (platforms.md, OI Directions) - likely not
  allowed. Artfi offered fractional art as NFTs (YourStory headline 2022-09-26 [S44]): crypto-like, grey.
- **Tax.** Art, watches, wine: capital assets; holding periods and rates for collectibles are not in the
  tax file: CA question.
