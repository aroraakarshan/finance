# Switzerland - quality and a haven currency, but expensive and taxed at source

Written 2026-11-15. Market research, not advice; the user decides. Sources [S#] at the end.
User context: 81% of Rs 2.23 Cr in QCOM + MSFT. Switzerland is pharma, food, banks and
insurers, priced in CHF, the strongest major currency against INR over 10 years.

## Summary
- Quality market (pharma, food, banks) and CHF, the best major currency vs INR over 10 years (+66%).
- But the dearest market in Europe (P/E 24 vs 10y avg 18.8) and Roche, Novartis, Nestle are 46% of the SMI.
- 35% Swiss withholding (treaty 10%, reclaim 25 pts); CSSMI/CHSPI are Swiss-domiciled, not Irish.
- Verdict: skip as a separate holding; hold it through a pan-Europe ETF; small SW2CHB (LU, acc) if he wants a haven.

## 7. Risks

- Valuation: P/E about 5 points above its 10-year average [S6].
- Concentration: three stocks are 46% of the SMI [S2]; a pharma-specific shock (drug
  pricing, patents) moves the whole market.
- A very strong franc hurts exporters' earnings; SNB has little room to cut (already 0%).
- Swiss withholding friction (section 8). No capital controls.

## 8. Dividend withholding and India DTAA

Switzerland withholds 35% on dividends; the India-Switzerland treaty leaves 10% (PwC,
reviewed 2026-07-01 [S3]; a 5% MFN rate for large holdings was withdrawn after the Indian
Supreme Court ruling of 19 Oct 2023). He would reclaim 25 points from the Swiss tax office
(ESTV); form and processing time for India residents not verified. FTC in India only for 10%.

Tax one-liners (tax/investment-types.md): foreign shares/ETFs LTCG 12.5% after 24 months,
else slab; dividends at slab with FTC for treaty-rate withholding; accumulating funds: no
tax until sale.

## 9. Best instruments for this user and verdict

1. Hold Switzerland through a pan-Europe accumulating ETF (MEUD or SMEA, about 15%
   Switzerland, see [eurozone.md](eurozone.md)): Swiss tax is paid inside the fund, so no
   reclaim paperwork for him.
2. **SW2CHB** (UBS MSCI Switzerland 20/35 Acc, LU0977261329, 0.20%): if he wants a separate
   CHF/defensive sleeve; capped weights reduce the Roche/Novartis/Nestle risk.
3. CHSPI (0.10%) is cheapest, but Swiss-domiciled and distributing: expect 35% withheld on
   payouts and a reclaim each year. Avoid CSSMI (0.35%, 20 stocks).

**Verdict: skip as a separate holding (neutral inside a Europe ETF).** Good quality and the
best currency against INR, but the highest P/E in Europe, 46% in three stocks, and a 35%
withholding hassle. If he wants a haven, a small SW2CHB position is the clean route.
