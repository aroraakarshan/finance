# Eurozone (and wider Europe) - the main non-US developed diversifier

Written 2026-11-15. Market research, not advice; the user decides. Sources: [eurozone/sources.md](eurozone/sources.md).

## Summary
- Europe is cheaper than the US and holds what he lacks (banks, industrials, utilities), paid in EUR.
- Verdict: overweight versus his current 0%, neutral versus world weights.
- Buy in steps through one accumulating pan-Europe ETF: MEUD (0.07%) or SMEA (0.12%).
- P/E is high versus Europe's own history and the 2025 defence/bank rally is fading: avoid chasing defence.

## Best instruments and verdict

1. **MEUD** (Amundi Core Stoxx Europe 600 Acc, LU0908500753, 0.07%, EUR 22.7 bn; USD line
   MEUS): broadest, cheapest, accumulating, includes UK and Switzerland. One-fund Europe.
2. **SMEA / IMEA** (iShares Core MSCI Europe Acc, IE00B4K48X80, 0.12%): same job, Irish.
3. **CSEMU / IEMU** (iShares Core MSCI EMU Acc, IE00B53QG562, 0.12%): only if he wants the
   eurozone without UK/Swiss.
4. Single stocks (ASML, SAP, banks, defence) only as small learning positions; withholding
   reclaims make dividend payers (Germany, Nordics) a chore.

**Verdict: overweight versus his current 0%, neutral versus world weights.** Europe is
cheaper than the US, holds what he lacks (banks, industrials, utilities) and pays in EUR.
But P/E is high versus its own history and 2025's defence/bank rally is fading, so build in
steps (no lump sum) through one accumulating pan-Europe ETF; avoid chasing defence.
