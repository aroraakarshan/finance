# Cyprus: property for an Indian resident

## Summary
Skip. Non-EU buyers need government approval and rules may tighten; title-deed delays are a known risk.
Gross yield 5.14%, low rent tax, entry costs 2.5-6%. PR needs EUR 300k (Rs 3.12 Cr) in new builds.
Minimum ~Rs 1.9 Cr.

## Details: Cyprus (Limassol, Nicosia)

**Can an Indian buy?** Yes, but a non-EU buyer needs approval (Council of Ministers) under the
Acquisition of Immovable Property (Aliens) Law; normally one home. Four bills to tighten
non-EU buying were deferred in April 2026 and are back in committee; MPs pushed again on
2026-10-22 (Cyprus Property News 2026-09-02; Cyprus Mail 2026-10-22). Non-EU buyers were 3,293
of 4,980 sale contracts in Jan-Jul 2026 (Cyprus Property News).

- **Buy costs:** transfer fee 1.5-4%, legal 1-2% -> 2.5-6% (GPG, Sep 2026). New builds carry
  VAT instead (rate for non-resident buyers not verified).
- **Running:** no national property tax since 2017; local fees only (GPG).
- **Tax (non-resident):** rent at progressive 0-35% after a 20% deduction, plus health levy
  (effective 2.65% of gross at EUR 1,500/month); gains 20% flat (GPG, Sep 2026).
- **Visa link:** fast-track permanent residency for EUR 300k (Rs 3.12 Cr) in up to two **new**
  homes from a developer, plus EUR 50k/yr foreign income (Nomad Capitalist 2026-07-03).
- **Liquidity/risk:** title-deed delays ("trapped buyers") are a known Cyprus problem (Cyprus Mail
  2026-06-10 headline); oversupply warning (Cyprus Mail 2026-10-12). Rules for non-EU buyers may
  tighten.
- **Verdict: skip** (maybe Nicosia for yield, but approval and title risk). Minimum ~Rs 1.9 Cr.
