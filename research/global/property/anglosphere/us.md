## Details

FX and sources: [basics.md](basics.md), [sources.md](sources.md).

**Can an Indian buy?** Yes, freehold, no federal ban. State limits target certain countries,
not India: Florida ch. 692 Part III "foreign countries of concern" = China, Russia, Iran, North
Korea, Cuba, Venezuela (Maduro regime), Syria (2026 Florida Statutes). Texas SB 17 (effective
1 Sep 2025) bans buyers tied to "designated countries" = countries named as a national-security
risk in the DNI threat assessments or designated by the governor; the bill text names China,
Russia, Iran, North Korea (Texas Legislature). India not named; later governor designations:
not verified.

**Purchase cost.** No foreign surcharge; closing costs and state transfer taxes vary, about
2-5% (not verified). **Running costs.** Property tax set locally, "varies widely" (PwC, Sep
2026); Texas/Florida rates, insurance and HOA fees not verified.

**Tax for a non-resident.** Rent: 30% of gross, or elect under IRC 871(d) to be taxed on net at
graduated rates (IRS). Sale: buyer withholds 15% of the gross price under FIRPTA (IRS); the gain
is taxed at up to 20% long-term (PwC). Texas and Florida have no state income tax (not verified).
**Estate tax:** a US home is US-situs; only USD 60,000 is exempt and rates reach 40%, no
India-US estate treaty - see [research/themes/us-estate-tax.md](../../../themes/us-estate-tax.md). A single-member LLC is normally
disregarded for tax, so it does not by itself remove the home from the estate (not verified
here; needs advice). The user already holds mostly US stock, which is also exposed.
**Visa.** None from a home (EB-5 needs a job-creating business; not re-checked).
**Liquidity/title.** Deep market, title insurance; wire fraud at closing is a known risk.
**Verdict: skip.** Adds more USD and more US estate-tax exposure to a portfolio already
concentrated in one US stock. Min budget about Rs 1.6 Cr (Texas condo + costs).
