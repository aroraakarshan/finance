# Global regions: valuation, performance and correlation

Back to the index: [opp-global-regions.md](../opp-global-regions.md). Written 2026-11-15.

## Summary
- US is the most expensive region (CAPE 39.95 vs long-run mean 17.45, multpl 13 Nov 2026); Europe and Japan sit near their own averages (forward P/E 14-16).
- EM looks cheap (forward P/E 8.7-9.7) only because of peak memory-chip earnings; on CAPE it is expensive.
- Momentum (to 13 Nov 2026): India and Europe up, US flat-up, Japan flat, EM down after the Oct-Nov 2026 chip selloff.
- India (0.37) and Europe (0.53) move least with QCOM+MSFT; EM ex-China (0.61) moves most with QCOM.
- What to do: favour regions that are both near-average in price and low in correlation (see the index ranking).

What this says (my reading):
- US: still the most expensive. CAPE about 40 is near the 1999 peak (44.19) and at the 96th
  percentile since 1980 (AQR, Dec 2025). Forward P/E fell from 22 to 19 in 2026 because earnings rose.
- Europe and Japan: close to their own long-run averages (Japan 15.6 vs about 15). AQR (Dec 2025):
  non-US developed CAPE is "near the historical median".
- EM: looks very cheap on forward P/E (8.7-9.7), but that is driven by a memory-chip earnings boom:
  Korea's 2026/2027 earnings forecasts were raised 183%/243% this year and Korea trades at about 4x
  forward P/E (JPMPB, 22 Oct 2026). Peak-cycle earnings make the P/E look low. On CAPE EM is expensive
  (98th percentile, AQR).
- India: forward P/E 18.4-18.6 is about 2x EM (my calculation from the rows above); JPM's average
  premium since 2007 is 1.4x (CNBC 6 Nov 2026). It has de-rated after a weak 2026.
- Discrepancies: iShares fund P/Es (12 Nov) are well above MSCI index P/Es (30 Oct), e.g. EM
  18.2 vs 13.7, US 28.7 vs 24.7. Different dates and methods; neither checked further. The GTM
  "now" vs "1 Jan 2026" labels are my reading of the chart, cross-checked with MSCI USA 18.7 and
  Eastspring Japan 15.6. 20-year averages are chart lines only: not verified.
