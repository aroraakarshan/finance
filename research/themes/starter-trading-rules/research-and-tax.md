# Starter trading rules: research and tax

Back to the index: [../starter-trading-rules.md](../starter-trading-rules.md).

## Summary
Most individual traders lose after costs: about 9 in 10 in Indian F&O (SEBI FY22-FY26), 7 in 10 in Indian intraday (SEBI, FY23).
The more often people trade, the worse they do (Barber & Odean 2000). So: small budget, few trades, strict loss limits.
Tax: Indian STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5%; US shares short-term at slab rate under 24 months; some items NOT VERIFIED, see table.

## Research on retail trading

| Study | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Barber & Odean (2000), "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth", Journal of Finance 55(2): 773-806 | 66,465 US households 1991-96. Most active traders earned 11.4% a year vs market 17.9%; average household 16.4%, turnover 75% a year | faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf |
| Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (2014), "The cross-section of speculator skill", Journal of Financial Markets 18: 1-24 | Taiwan 1992-2006: "Less than 1% of the day trader population is able to predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees" | faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf |
| Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (SSRN 3423101, 2019; FGV EESP WP 525, 2020) | Brazil mini-index futures day traders who kept going 300+ days: 97% lost money; current abstract: only 0.4% earned more than a bank teller (US$54 a day). Takeaway: the more often individuals trade, the worse they tend to do after costs. A small
budget, few trades and strict loss limits limit the damage while skill is tested.

## Tax and cost notes (short)

The Income-tax Act, 2025 is in force from 2026-04-01 (incometax.gov.in homepage, seen
2026-11-15). Section numbers below are from the 1961 Act; rates are from Budget 2024 and
were not re-checked against Budget 2026. Confirm with a tax adviser or `finance-tax`.

| Item | Rule | Source / status |
|---|---|---|
| India listed equity, held up to 12 months | STCG 20% (s.111A), for transfers from 2024-07-23 | Budget 2024 memorandum, indiabudget.gov.in/budget2024-25/doc/memo.pdf - verified |
| India listed equity, over 12 months | LTCG 12.5% above Rs 1.25 L a year (s.112A), no indexation | Same memorandum - verified |
| Intraday equity (India) | Speculative business income, at slab (s.43(5)); losses set off only against speculative income | ITR-3 has a speculative business line (incometax.gov.in ITR-3 validation rules AY 2026-27); s.43(5) wording NOT VERIFIED |
| US stocks for an Indian resident | Not listed in India -> long-term after 24 months, LTCG 12.5% no indexation; under 24 months at slab rate | 24-month rule for non-listed assets verified in Budget 2024 memo; applying it to foreign-listed shares is inferred, NOT VERIFIED from the Act |
| Schedule FA | Foreign shares and accounts must be reported in the ITR | Requirement verified (ITR rules); calendar-year basis NOT VERIFIED here |
| TCS on LRS remittance | Threshold raised from Rs 7 L to Rs 10 L per year (Budget speech 2025-02-01, para 138); 20% above threshold for investment remittances | Threshold verified as Budget proposal; 2025-04-01 start and 20% rate above threshold NOT VERIFIED from CBDT (INDmoney page says so, secondary) |
| Trading costs | INDmoney fees $158.30 on $59,500 traded (0.27%), plus spread and FX conversion (not measured) | Script run 2026-11-15 |

Short-term US gains at slab rate (likely 30% plus cess for a salaried person; slab not checked)
cost more than Indian STCG at 20%; frequent US trading is taxed harder.
