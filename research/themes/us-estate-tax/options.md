# US estate tax: Ways to reduce exposure

Written 2026-11-15 by `finance-research`. Research only, not tax or legal advice; no orders placed.
Part of [US estate tax](../us-estate-tax.md). Sources read 2026-11-15 unless another date is given; "not verified" = no source confirmed it.
Short names: "IRS page" = https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/some-nonresidents-with-us-assets-must-file-estate-tax-returns ; "i706na" = Form 706-NA instructions, https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na (rev. 09/2025).

## Summary
Main levers: move US shares into Irish UCITS ETFs (sale taxed in India), sell new MSFT vests at
once, and buy term cover to pay the tax. T-bills only if LRS-permitted and a CA agrees. Joint holding,
nominees and a TOD do not help; a holding company is not advisable.

## Details

| Option | Effect on estate tax | Cost / trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Switch US single stocks/ETFs to Ireland-domiciled UCITS ETFs at IBKR (e.g. CSPX = iShares Core S&P 500, ISIN IE00B5BMR087, domicile Ireland, TER 0.07%, accumulating, iShares page https://www.ishares.com/uk/individual/en/products/253743/ishares-sp-500-b-ucits-etf-acc-fund ; VUAA, VWRA, SWDA domicile not checked) | Removes them from the US estate (non-US stock, 2104(a)) | Indian tax on the sale: foreign shares are long-term after 24 months at 14.95% (12.5% + 15% surcharge + cess), short-term at 35.88%, no Rs 1.25 L exemption; selling all QCOM now adds about Rs 5.8-5.9 L tax, all MSFT (all short-term) about Rs 2.2-2.5 L (`tax/investment-types.md` sections 0, 5.1). Accumulating UCITS: no Indian tax until sale (no deemed-income rule found) and the same 24-month/14.95% rule on sale; bond UCITS may be always short-term under AF s.76 (section 1). Do not lend the UCITS via IBKR SYEP: it may count as a sale and reset the holding period (IB India clients not eligible anyway; section 0 item 2). No US capital gains tax for an NRNC present in the US under 183 days (26 USC 871(a)(2)). Loses single-stock upside/concentration; LSE trading in USD/GBP lines, wider spreads (not measured). |
| (a) dividend side | - | US dividends to an Indian resident: 30% statutory (871(a)(1)), treaty cap 25% for portfolio holders (India-US treaty Art. 10(2)(b), https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/india.pdf). US dividends to an Irish fund: 15% treaty rate for portfolio dividends (Ireland-US treaty, Art. 10, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/ireland.pdf); whether each fund gets it is not verified. On a ~1.2% S&P yield (not verified) that saves about 0.12%/yr. Ireland withholds 0% on UCITS distributions to an Indian resident, and US-listed ETFs/stocks suffer 25% US withholding with W-8BEN (`tax/investment-types.md` sections 0 item 6, 1; marked [IR] there). Distributing UCITS payouts are taxed at slab (35.88%) in India. |
| (b) Conditional: US Treasuries / T-bills instead of broker cash or US money market funds, only if LRS-permitted and after a CA check | Not US-situs (2105(b)(3),(4)) | LRS status is GREY: direct US bonds/T-bills trade OTC and may fall outside permitted "listed" OPI (`research/themes/ibkr-global/cash-and-bonds.md` section 3.3, not verified). Must buy the bills directly (US MMF shares are US-situs). No US tax (portfolio interest / short-term OID). Indian tax: interest at slab 35.88%; a T-bill sale is always short-term; notes/bonds may be "unlisted bonds" that are always short-term under AF s.76; US MMFs probably always short-term too (`tax/investment-types.md` sections 0 item 1, 1). So, even if allowed, T-bills are no better on Indian tax than cash interest (also 35.88%). Not a recommendation; only an idea to check with a CA (LRS and AF s.76). |
| (c) Term life insurance (Indian insurer) sized to the liability | Does not cut the tax; pays it. Proceeds on an NRNC's life are not US-situs (2105(a)) | Indicative Rs 1 Cr cover to age 60, healthy non-smoking male: about Rs 10,508/yr at age 35, Rs 13,738/yr at 40 (Policybazaar, https://www.policybazaar.com/life-insurance/term-insurance/1-crore-term-insurance/ , secondary, date not shown). User's age and health not known. About Rs 1 Cr of cover would match the ~Rs 82.5 L tax with unvested awards (Rs 43.3 L on current assets alone). |
| (d) Hold via a non-US company | Shares of a foreign company are non-US (2104(a)) | Not advisable for an individual: set-up and yearly cost, Indian ODI/LRS rules (resident individuals face limits on ODI into financial-services entities - not verified here, check RBI Overseas Investment Directions 2022 at rbi.org.in), Indian tax on the company (not verified), and the US may look through a sham company (not verified). |
| (e) Joint holding or nominees | No help: full value of joint property with survivorship is generally included (i706na "Entire gross estate"); nominee-held US stock is still US-situs (IRS page above) | - |
| (f) Employer stock (MSFT RSU/ESPP) | Each vest adds US-situs value at Fidelity; unvested awards ($124,683 gross) already count, because they vest at death ([exposure.md](exposure.md)). Selling at vest cannot remove them while employed | Selling at vest gives about zero gain (Indian cost = FMV at vest), so little Indian tax, and moving the money to (a) keeps the US estate flat (`tax/employer-stock.md`). Existing MSFT lots are a different case: see [proposal.md](proposal.md). |
| (g) Indian assets for new money instead of more US assets | Not US-situs | Per `tax/investment-types.md` section 2: SGB redemption is exempt only for original subscribers held to maturity (FA 2026), so secondary-market SGBs lose it; tax-free bonds pay exempt interest; other interest at 30% slab (35.88%); NPS own Rs 50k (80CCD(1B)) old regime only, employer 14% allowed in the new regime. |
| (h) Irish CAT check for UCITS | Irish UCITS fund units passing between Indian residents are exempt from Irish capital acquisitions tax under s.75 CATCA 2003 (neither disponer nor successor domiciled or ordinarily resident in Ireland) | `legal/estate/foreign-assets.md` s.4.13 (Revenue CAT Manual Part 9, https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/capital-acquisitions-tax/cat-manual/part-09-exemptions/part-9-exemptions.pdf). Not covered: gold ETCs such as IGLN (debt securities, not fund units) and an heir living in Ireland. Whether an Irish grant of probate is needed: not verified. |
| Gifts during life | Gift tax does not apply to gifts of intangibles (e.g. shares) by an NRNC (26 USC 2501(a)(2), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/2501) | Gift to a spouse moves the problem to the spouse if also NRNC; transfers within 3 years or revocable ones can be pulled back in (2104(b)); Indian gift tax/clubbing rules not checked. Get advice before using. |
