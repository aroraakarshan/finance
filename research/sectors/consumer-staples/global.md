# Consumer staples: global, Japan and other Asia

Written 2026-11-15 by `finance-research`. Market research, not advice; no orders. Prices are
13-Nov-2026 closes. Source keys are in section 7. Back to the index: [README.md](README.md).

## Summary
- Global staples (food, drinks, household goods) jumped about 25% in a year (XDWS in EUR [JE]) and
  11% in the last month as investors moved out of tech; US staples now cost 23.7x next-year
  earnings vs a 10-year average of 20.1 [FS].
- Weight-loss drugs (GLP-1) are a real, measured drag: US households on them buy 5.3% less
  groceries and 10.1% fewer savoury snacks [JMR]; beer and spirits volumes are also weak.
- Not a good time for the whole basket; pick the cheaper turnarounds (Unilever, forward P/E about
  18-20) and keep XDWS for staged buying only.

## 5. Key risks
- **GLP-1 weight-loss drugs.** A Journal of Marketing Research study (Apr-2026) linking surveys to a
  US household panel found: grocery spending -5.3% within 6 months (-8.2% for high-income homes),
  savoury snacks -10.1%, fast food and coffee shops -8.0%; yogurt was the main winner; the effect
  fades after 6 months and reverses when people stop [JMR]. Most exposed: snacks (PepsiCo), sweets,
  beer and spirits (Diageo, AB InBev). Less exposed: home and personal care (P&G, Kao, Unilever
  after the foods split).
- **Alcohol.** Younger people drink less and GLP-1 adds to it; Diageo North America -3.6% [FM], [INV].
- **Valuation.** US staples at 23.7x vs 20.1 average [FS]: a rotation back to tech would hit them.
- **Input costs and price cuts.** PepsiCo had to cut prices up to 15% and still lost volume [YH4].
- **Currency.** All returns above are in local currency; INR moves change the result for an Indian buyer.
