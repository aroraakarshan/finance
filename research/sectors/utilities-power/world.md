## Summary
- US power demand is rising for the first time in decades because of AI data centres; power producers
  with nuclear plants (Constellation, Vistra) were the big winners in 2023-24 but fell 16-22% in the last year.
- US utilities now cost about as much as the whole market; European ones rose 25% in a year; Japan's
  Kansai Electric and Hong Kong's CLP are the calmer, cheaper options.
- Buy XDWU (Irish world utilities fund) as the core; add National Grid, Kansai or CLP as single names.
  You already hold CEG and VRT: do not add more AI-power names.

## US and AI power demand
- Forecasts: BloombergNEF (2026-07-21): data centres use 5.9% of US power today, about 12% by 2030 and 20% by
  2035; US data-centre demand 194 GW by 2035, 83% above its December estimate [W6]. IEA via Our World in
  Data: data centres used about 485 TWh in 2025 (about 1.5% of world power), about 3% by 2030 [W7].
- Prices: PJM (the grid of 13 eastern states) held its capacity auction (payments for being available at
  peak) in July 2026; it cleared at the price cap and 6.8 GW short of the reliability target (headlines)
  [W8]. The market monitor tied billions of dollars of these costs to data centres and says 42% of forecast
  data-centre load is delayed or cancelled (headlines, bodies not read) [W8].
- Constellation (Q3 2026, 2026-11-09): adjusted EPS $5.06 vs $3.04 a year ago; 2026 guidance raised to
  $12.50-12.80; Google deal for new nuclear capacity; conditional US DOE loan up to $5bn for reactor upgrades
  [W9]. Its share price is still 16% below a year ago because the AI-power trade cooled.
- What this means for you: CEG (and VRT) already give you this bet; they move with the same AI cycle as QCOM
  and MSFT (theme note: AI-power funds have 0.45-0.59 correlation with QCOM [W4]). Regulated utilities
  (Southern, NextEra's Florida utility) and XDWU spread the bet with less swing.

## Europe
- Iberdrola: nine-month adjusted profit +9% to EUR 5.1bn; 2026 growth target raised to at least 10%;
  regulated grid assets to grow from EUR 56bn to over EUR 90bn by 2030 [W10]. Strong, but fwd P/E 21.3 after +36%.
- National Grid: half-year to Sep 2026 underlying operating profit +13% to GBP 2.59bn, record GBP 6.7bn
  investment (+33%), underlying EPS +22% to 36.5p, interim dividend 16.97p; first year of the new UK price
  control (RIIO-T3) [W11]. More debt as it spends.
- RWE: results above consensus and a joint venture with KKR closed (headline, 2026-11-11) [W12]; a Jul-Sep
  loss in Yahoo's data makes its trailing P/E useless. Enel: cheapest on forward P/E (13.0) and 4.9% yield;
  no recent news read.

## Japan
- Kansai Electric: a nuclear-heavy Japanese utility. First-half (Apr-Sep 2026) profit fell on higher
  fuel costs and lower nuclear output (headlines) [W13]; Jul-Sep profit JPY 45.9bn vs 133.8bn a year earlier
  [W1]. It raised its dividend and the Kansai region is drawing data centres (headlines) [W13]. Cheap: P/E 9.2,
  below book value.
- Tokyo Electric (TEPCO): restarted Kashiwazaki-Kariwa unit 6, commercial operation from April 2026 after a
  stop-start January-March; plans $20bn of cost cuts over 10 years (headlines) [W14]. The share halved in a
  year; the main cause is not verified. Fukushima costs and a JPY 454bn loss in FY to Mar 2026 [W1]. Avoid.
