# India healthcare services: hospitals, diagnostics, pharmacies, health insurance

Written 2026-11-15 by `finance-research`. Market research, not advice; no orders. Prices to 13-Nov-2026.
Pharma and medtech are in [pharma.md](pharma.md) and [biotech-medtech.md](biotech-medtech.md); the global
health care ETF case is in [opp-sectors-themes](../../themes/opp-sectors-themes.md). Source keys [S#] at the end.

## Summary
- Indian hospital revenue rose 17-34% on a year earlier (Sep-2026 quarter) on new beds, higher occupancy and more foreign patients.
- They are expensive: 20-37x EV/EBITDA, 2-3x Asian peers IHH and Bangkok Dusit (12.5x), and the Nifty Healthcare P/E is the highest in 5 years.
- But most hospital stocks fell or stalled in the last year and now trade at or below their own 6-10 year median multiples.
- Health insurance grew 15% after GST on personal policies went to 0% (Sep 2025); medical inflation of 12-14% is the main risk.
- If buying: small, in steps, cheapest first: Narayana Health, Star Health, Apollo; you already hold 5 ICICI Lombard shares.

## 4. Diagnostics and pharmacies
- Price competition: Amazon (with Orange Health), Tata 1mg, PharmEasy and Apollo 24/7 sell cheap home tests
  (Livemint, 23-Jun-2025 [S16]). So far the big labs' margins rose, not fell: Dr Lal Q2 revenue +19.6%,
  EBITDA margin 32.1% (30.7%), profit +27.6% [S17]; Metropolis revenue +16%, profit +43%; Vijaya profit +37%;
  Thyrocare revenue +25.1% but profit fell on a Rs 45 cr write-down; it sold its radiology unit for Rs 141 cr
  (headlines [S18]). Dr Lal: Goldman Sachs still sees valuation risk (headline, 4-Nov-2026 [S18]).
- Apollo HealthCo (pharmacies and the Apollo 24/7 app): Q2 revenue Rs 3,286 cr (+23%), profit Rs 118 cr,
  7,662 stores [S6]. It is to be demerged and listed with Keimed (wholesale) at ~Rs 25,000 cr yearly revenue,
  target Dec-2026 / early 2027 (FE, 21-May-2026 [S19]); current timing not verified.
- MedPlus: Q2 revenue +21% to Rs 2,037 cr, profit flat; Nomura cut its target to Rs 915 (headlines,
  27-30 Oct 2026 [S18]). Low-margin retail (9.4% EBITDA margin); stock -21% in a year.

## 5. Health insurance and the GST cut
- From 22-Sep-2025 personal health policies pay 0% GST instead of 18% [S20]. Health premiums reached a record
  Rs 1.37 L cr in FY26 (+15%); retail +20% (FE, 20-Apr-2026 [S21]).
- Costs for insurers: they lose input tax credit (GST paid on their own costs), about 3-4% of costs
  (brokers' body, Livemint 4-Sep-2025 [S22]). Medical inflation runs 12-14% a year (Reuters, 12-Aug-2026 [S23]);
  some customers saw renewal premiums up 25-41% a year after the cut (moneycontrol, Oct-2026 [S24]).
- Star Health Q2 FY27: premiums +19%, new retail premiums +35%, underwriting profit Rs 99 cr (loss Rs 40 cr),
  claims-plus-costs ratio 98.0% (100.9%) (company release, 30-Oct-2026 [S25]).
- ICICI Lombard Q2 FY27: profit +15% to Rs 946 cr, but direct premiums +3% vs industry +7%; combined ratio
  105.2% in H1 (103%); health is 37% of its book, motor 39% (FE, 14-Oct-2026 [S26]). You hold 5 shares at AngelOne
  (`portfolio/details.md`).
- Room to grow: insurance is 3.7% of GDP vs 7.3% globally; non-life only 1% (McKinsey, Sep-2026 [S27]).

## 6. Government and Asian peers
- Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY, free cover for poorer families): 43.5 cr cards, 36,000+ hospitals (Mar-2026 [S28]).
  Its fixed package rates are low and payments slow: 2,359 hospitals removed and 1,200 suspended (Aug-2026),
  Haryana hospitals halted service over Rs 1,200 cr dues (Sep-2026) (headlines [S28]). Big chains rely on it
  less than small-city hospitals (48% of revenue there, YourStory Nov-2025 [S28]); chain shares not verified.
- Price rules: a parliamentary panel wants standard package rates and clear prices (11-Aug-2026 [S29]); an
  IRDAI-led panel is studying benchmark treatment rates, a common product and a national claims exchange,
  with proposals due by end-2026 [S23]. Capped prices are the largest single risk to hospital margins.

## 7. Growth outlook and risks
- Drivers: India healthcare ~10.6% a year (IHH, Sep-2026 [S9]); more insured people; few beds per person
  (not verified); complex care; foreign patients. Consensus EPS growth FY27 to FY28: Apollo +27%, Max +24%,
  Fortis +34%, Narayana +33%, Dr Lal +16%, Star +22%, ICICI Lombard +23% (Yahoo, 12-27 analysts [S2]).
- Risks: price caps and package-rate rules; Ayushman payment delays; new hospitals take 2-3 years to fill and
  cut margins meanwhile (KIMS P/E 144); staff costs (new labour code, Nov-2025 [S9]); Narayana's UK business;
  claims inflation for insurers; at 35-50x earnings any slowdown hits prices hard.
