A REIT (real estate investment trust) owns buildings that earn rent and must pay out most of its income.

## Summary
- Global REITs are fairly priced, not cheap: US real estate trades at 18.0x next year's earnings vs its 10-year average of 18.6 and the S&P 500's 18.3 [R17].
- The headwind is rates: the US 10-year bond pays 5.15% (up from about 4.1% a year ago), more than the 3.5% REIT dividend yield [R1].
- Best area: Japan. Tokyo office vacancy is 1.75% and rents have risen 33 months in a row [R24]; Mitsui Fudosan is at 15x forecast profit and barely moves with QCOM/MSFT.
- Data-centre REITs (Equinix, Digital Realty) are growing fast but expensive and partly the same AI bet you already hold.
- Buy: HPRA (global REITs, Irish, accumulating, 0.24%) as the core; Mitsui Fudosan; CICT in Singapore for income.

## 3. Rate sensitivity
- REITs borrow a lot and are bought for income, so they fall when bond yields rise. The US 10-year
  yield went from about 4.1% to 5.15% in a year [R1]; US REITs still returned 12%, helped by data centres.
- US REIT dividend yield (VNQ 3.5%) is below the 10-year bond (5.15%); listed REITs' implied cap rate
  (rent / property value) was 5.74% vs 4.47% for private appraisals in Q2 2026 (Nareit, via [R22]).
  So listed REITs are priced below private property values, but give little extra over bonds.
- Japan: J-REIT yield 5.2% vs JGB 2.84%: a 2.4-point cushion, but JGB yields rising pushed J-REITs down 17% in a year [R1][R25].
- India: REIT yields 5-7% vs a 7.32% 10-year G-sec, and the RBI is now hiking ([REIT note](../../themes/opp-real-assets/reits.md)).
- Discrepancy: the REIT note says the 10-year (5.15%) is "above the REIT cap rate"; it is above the
  private-appraisal rate (4.47%) but below the listed-REIT rate (5.74%).

## 4. Japan: J-REITs and developers
- Tokyo's 5 central wards: office vacancy 1.75% in Oct 2026 (7th monthly fall); asking rent JPY 24,245
  per tsubo, up for 33 months in a row (Miki Shoji) [R24]. Tight market = rising rents for landlords.
- **Mitsui Fudosan (8801.T)**, Japan's largest developer (offices, LaLaport malls, homes, hotels, logistics,
  Tokyo Dome): H1 FY3/27 operating profit -19.5% on lower property-sale gains, but leasing profit
  +JPY 10.9 bn; full-year forecast kept: net profit JPY 285 bn (a record) [R23].
  - Price JPY 1,623; forecast EPS = 285 bn / 2.69 bn shares = JPY 105.9 -> **fwd P/E 15.3**; trailing
    P/E 16.6 (EPS 97.61), P/B 1.30, yield 2.25% [R20][R1]. Debt is high (JPY 5.0 trillion) [R20].
  - Correlation with QCOM -0.10, MSFT -0.06 (5y monthly) [R1]: the best diversifier in this note.
  - One lot (100 shares) = JPY 162,300, about Rs 95,400 (JPYINR 0.5879) [R1].
- Mitsubishi Estate (8802.T) rose 25% in a year (price) [R1]; valuation not pulled.
- J-REIT ETF NEXT FUNDS TSE REIT (1343.T): yield 5.2%, -17% price in a year [R1]: a yield buy if
  you expect JGB yields to stop rising.

## 7. Outlook and risks
- **Drivers:** AI data-centre demand (Equinix and Digital Realty raised 2026 guidance 3 times); warehouse
  rents still re-pricing up; Tokyo office shortage; HK prices expected to recover.
- **Risks:** (1) bond yields keep rising (US 10y 5.15%, JGB 10y up); (2) refinancing old cheap debt
  costs more (S-REITs) [R26]; (3) US offices (BXP 52% below its high) and European landlords (Vonovia,
  German homes, 62% below) [R1]; (4) data-centre REITs could fall with AI stocks (same risk as your tech
  holdings); (5) currency: the rupee fell 6% vs the USD in a year, which lifted foreign returns in
  rupees; the reverse can happen [R1].
