# Consumer discretionary: autos, retail, luxury, travel

Written 2026-11-15 by `finance-research`. Market research, not about the current portfolio; no orders.
"Consumer discretionary" = things people buy when they have spare money (cars, bikes, clothes,
jewellery, online shopping, hotels), as opposed to staples (food, soap). Prices: yfinance to
2026-11-13 [D1]. Every number has a tag; tags are listed in [sources](sources.md).
Wider sector ranking (health care, energy and others): [opp-sectors-themes](../../themes/opp-sectors-themes.md).

## Summary

- India autos are booming after the GST cut of 22 Sep 2025: record October 2026 (cars +12.4%, two-wheelers +16.1%) [D10]; Nifty Auto P/E 31.0 sits at its 10-year median 31.2 [D2]. Fair, not cheap.
- Global luxury is in a slump (LVMH -36% this year, Hermes Q3 growth weakest since 2020) [D6][D24]: cheap vs its own history, but China has not turned yet.
- US discretionary is dear (forward P/E 24.3 vs 25-year average 20.3) and is mostly Amazon + Tesla [D3][D5], which overlaps the tech you already hold.
- If you add: a Nifty Auto ETF in rupees, LVMH as a small contrarian buy, Toyota for value and dividend. Buy in 2-3 steps over 6 months.
- Skip for now: Tesla (P/E ~480), quick commerce (Swiggy loss-making, Eternal P/E ~680) and the high-P/E Indian retailers (Trent 89, DMart 72) [D1].
