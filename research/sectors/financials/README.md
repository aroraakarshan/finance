## Summary
- Indian banks are the cheapest in 10 years against their own net worth (Nifty Bank P/B 1.84 vs 10-yr average 2.61), after RBI's first rate hike in 3+ years (repo 5.50%, 7 Oct 2026) and with new loan-loss rules (ECL) from 1 Apr 2027. Profits still grow, so this looks like a good time to buy slowly.
- Banks abroad have already run hard (Europe and Japan bank funds about +200% in 3 years) and US financials trade above their long-run P/E. Own them through one broad fund, do not chase.
- What to buy: a Nifty Bank ETF in steps (India), XDWF (world financials, Irish fund) for the global part, and Munich Re (cheap, high-return insurer). Skip expensive Indian NBFCs, AMCs and BSE/CDSL for now.

## Key words (plain)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P/B (price to book) | Share price / the bank's net worth per share. Below 1 = market pays less than the books say |
| ROE | Profit / net worth. A bank earning 15% ROE deserves a higher P/B than one earning 8% |
| ECL | "Expected credit loss": banks set money aside for loans that MAY go bad, not only for ones already bad |
| Repo rate | Rate at which RBI lends to banks; it drives loan and deposit rates |
| NBFC | Non-bank lender (Bajaj Finance, Shriram); borrows from banks and markets, so pays more for money |
| UCITS | EU fund rules; Irish (and Luxembourg) UCITS funds are not hit by US estate tax |
