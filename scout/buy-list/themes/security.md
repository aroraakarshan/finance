## (a) Cybersecurity: steady growth, already in the price
- Demand: Gartner puts 2026 security spending at $247.5 bn, +12.7% in constant currency, and +11.2% in 2027. Securing AI is +83% but only $2.8 bn [S1].
  The median CISO budget grew 0% for the second year (IANS, Sep 2026) [S1].
- Leaders still grow 20-35%: CrowdStrike ARR $5.84 bn (+25%); net new ARR +51% in Q2 FY27, guide raised to +34% [S2]. Zscaler revenue +25%, Cloudflare +39% [Y].
  Check Point revenue fell 1% in Q3 2026 (subscriptions +11%); management calls it the trough [S3].
- Early or late: late. It is a 15-year trend. CrowdStrike trades at 42x sales and 139x forward EPS. Implied growth 64%/yr, against 25% revenue growth.
  Cheap names are cheap because they are not growing: Check Point (fwd P/E 13.4, implied 3%/yr), Trend Micro (fwd 24.3, net cash JPY 220 bn = 23% of its value) [Y].
- What kills it: Microsoft and the platforms bundle security (price pressure); a CrowdStrike-style outage; AI agents cut seat counts; stock-based pay (GAAP losses at ZS, NET, S).
- Base rate (2014-2018 IPOs that still trade, from first close) [Y]: Okta 7.6x, Zscaler 5.8x, CyberArk +700% from its IPO by 2024 [S4] and later bought by Palo Alto.
  Varonis 3.3x in 12 years, Tenable 1.4x, Rapid7 0.6x. SentinelOne (2021) 0.3x. 3 of 6 multiplied, but these are only the survivors, so the true rate is lower.

## (b) Defence: orders keep rising, share prices have reset
- Budgets: NATO allies agreed to spend 5% of GDP by 2035 (3.5% core defence). European allies plus Canada will spend about $634 bn in 2026, 2.53% of GDP [S5].
  Only 5 allies reach 3.5% in 2026 [S6], so spending can still grow by about 40% as a share of GDP.
  India FY27: MoD Rs 7.85 lakh Cr (+15%); capital acquisition Rs 1.85 lakh Cr (+24%), 75% of it reserved for Indian makers [S7].
  Japan FY27 request: a record JPY 8.9 trn, the last year of the JPY 43 trn 5-year plan (2% of GDP) [S8].
- Orders (latest quarter): Rheinmetall backlog EUR 78.0 bn (+38%), 9M sales +38%, but its 2026 sales guide was cut to EUR 13.2 bn [S9].
  Hensoldt order intake +77% (book-to-bill 1.9), backlog EUR 10.4 bn (+46%) [S10]. Leonardo orders +28%, backlog EUR 60.4 bn [S11].
  THEON 9M revenue +37%, Q3 +40% at a 26% EBIT margin; target now above +20% a year [S12]. BEL order book Rs 68,720 Cr (2.3x sales), Q2 revenue +26.5% [S13].
- Early or late: the shares are late, the orders are mid-cycle. From the eve of the invasion (23 Feb 2022) to their highs: Rheinmetall 21.6x, Mitsubishi Heavy 16.9x, Saab 14.3x, Leonardo 10.6x, HAL 8.8x, BEL 7.4x [Y].
  Now Rheinmetall is -61% from its high, Renk -63%, Kratos -74%; Saab and Kongsberg are only -14% to -19% [Y]. The Indian defence index has the highest P/E of all Indian sectors (56) and finance-research rates it avoid (research/sectors/ranking/india.md).
- What kills it: a Ukraine ceasefire that cuts ammunition orders; slow EU and German contract awards (Rheinmetall has cut guidance twice); Japan's budget flat after the plan ends in FY27; India's lumpy orders (Data Patterns Q2 revenue -57% [S14]).
- Base rate, the 1980s build-up: General Dynamics rose 82% in 1983 and 23% in 1984, then went nowhere in 1985-86 and fell 29% in 1987 and 43% in 1990. Over 1980-90 it was -18% [S15].
  Shares peaked about when budgets stopped growing (about 1985), years before spending fell. Only 1 name was checked, so this is a weak base rate.

## (c) Space: real revenue growth, peak prices at the SpaceX listing
- Growth is real: Rocket Lab Q3 revenue $268 m (+73%), backlog $3.57 bn (+52% in one quarter), 16 Neutron launches booked before its first flight [S16].
  MDA Space Q3 revenue +21.5%, 2026 guide raised to C$1.90-1.95 bn. But its backlog fell to C$3.9 bn from C$4.4 bn a year ago, so it is booking less than it delivers [S17].
  SpaceX revenue: $27.1 bn in 9M 2026 (xAI included); it spent $48.1 bn against $13.1 bn of cash from operations [S18].
- Early or late: late. SpaceX listed on 12 Jun 2026 at $1.78 trn, the largest IPO on record [S18]. Rocket Lab, AST, Planet, MDA and Redwire all peaked on 28 May 2026, and are now 40-79% below that peak [Y].
  SPCX is at $137.36, below its first close of $160.95 [Y]. Rocket Lab is buying Iridium (announced Jun 2026) [S19].
- What kills it: Starship lowers launch prices for everyone; satellite broadband becomes Starlink's alone; delays to Neutron; share sales by loss-making firms.
- Base rate, the 2021 space SPACs (SpaceQ, 16 deals, 20 Oct 2026) [S19]: 3 succeeded (Rocket Lab, Iridium, Intuitive Machines), 3 mixed (AST, Planet, Redwire) and 10 failed (Virgin Orbit bankrupt, Astra, Momentus, Virgin Galactic, Spire, BlackSky, Satellogic, Arqit, Terran, SatixFy).
  Multiplied from $10: Rocket Lab 5.1x, Iridium 4.4x, AST 4.2x (Intuitive Machines only 1.1x). All 3 first fell below $10 by year 3, and all 6 deals with under $10 m of revenue failed.
