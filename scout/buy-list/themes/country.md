What it means: demand and profits are accelerating, and parts making is new (PCBs, ECMS). The shares, though,
re-rated in 2023-24. Most leaders trade at P/E 55-115, and these prices assume about 30-45% EPS growth a year for
2-3 years (POWERINDIA research: about 45%/yr just to hold its price). The theme is mid-cycle, not 2022-Nvidia early.
Over the last year the market punished misses hard: KAYNES -53%, DIXON -16%, AMBER -12%.
Kill risks: the phone PLI ended (watch Dixon's margins in FY27); Chinese dumping (fluorochemicals, electronics);
US tariffs; slower smart-meter orders (Kaynes); working capital and accounting quality (Kaynes restated
revenue, inventory and receivables [I11]); not enough power for data centres; a de-rating from P/E 60-100.

What it means: the easy part is done. The cheapest, worst group already rose the most. Big contractors (Obayashi
+194%, Shimizu +147%, Taisei +138% in 3 years) and banks have re-rated. Many names still below book are cheap
because their own profits are weak: Suzuken says its 9% ROE includes one-off gains from selling shareholdings and
its underlying ROE is about 6% [J11]; Denso expects ROE to fall to 7.2% [J6]. Upside is a P/B move from 0.8-0.9 to
1.0-1.3 plus buybacks, roughly 1.3-1.6x, not a multi-bagger.
Kill risks: the yen strengthens from USD/JPY 159 (hurts exporters such as Denso); reform fatigue once P/B passes 1;
ROE that came from one-off sales of shareholdings; US car tariffs.
