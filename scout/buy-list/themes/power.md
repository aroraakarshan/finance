## (a) Grid and electrification: real demand, prices mostly already paid
- Demand still growing. GE Vernova Q3 2026: orders $29.3 bn (+96% organic), revenue +27% (+18% organic), backlog above $200 bn expected in Q4; gas turbines under contract 122 GW, at least 130 GW by end-2026 [S1].
  Siemens Energy FY9/2026: orders EUR 68.1 bn (+18.8%), revenue +15.5%, record backlog EUR 164 bn. FY27 guide: revenue +14-16%, Grid +22-24%, Gas Services +24-26%, net income about EUR 5 bn (FY26 4.1) [S2].
  Hitachi Q2 FY26: revenue +15%, Energy driven by HVDC awards in Europe and data-centre orders; full-year outlook raised [S3].
- Shortage and pricing: large transformers take 30-36 months (12-18 before the shortage), up to 60 months for extra-high voltage. US producer prices: transformers +91.4% (Jul 2026), switchgear +89.4% (Aug 2026) [S4]; step-up transformers above 160 weeks in Q1 2026 [S5].
- Why it is late, not early: shares already ran (Siemens Energy +1,304% in 3 years, GVT&D +1,039%, Hitachi Energy India +623%) and peaked in April (GE Vernova -33% and Siemens Energy -30% since then) [Y].
  First slowdowns: Powell (US switchgear) revenue growth fell from 45% (FY9/24) to 9% (FY9/25); NKT revenue flat year on year; GVT&D backlog flat in H1 FY27 [Y] [S6]. Record lead times usually come near the top of a capacity cycle, and makers are adding factories [S6].
- Priced in: Siemens Energy (P/E 21.9 on the FY27 guide) needs 13%/yr EPS growth, below its own guide. Prysmian needs 7%/yr. GE Vernova (29.3 on 2027 consensus) needs 20%/yr, Hitachi (27.5) 19%/yr. GVT&D (53) needs 35%/yr.
- What kills it: book-to-bill below 1 for 2 quarters at GE Vernova or Siemens Energy; transformer prices or lead times falling; hyperscalers cutting capex; customers cancelling gas-turbine reservations; cheap Chinese and Korean transformer imports.
- Base rate (telecom equipment, 2000 top, 6 leaders) [Y]: Cisco took 26 years to regain its high (Dec 2025 [S12]) and Corning 25. Ciena is -70%, JDSU (now Viavi) -95%, Nokia -86%, Ericsson -91%. 0 of 6 multiplied from the top.

## (b) Nuclear and fuel chain: the hype burst, the fuel business is real but slow
- Fuel: uranium spot $90.55/lb on 17 Nov 2026, +19% in a year, 1-year range $75.78-101.55 [S7].
  Kazatomprom 9M26: production +10-12%, sales +10%, average price received +17% to $73.51/lb; 2026 guidance kept [S8].
  Cameco Q3 2026: uranium EBITDA $179 m (Q3 2025 $220 m); deliveries contracted at over 28 m lb a year for 5 years, but fewer in 2029-30 [S9].
  Enrichment: Centrus backlog $7.0 bn to 2040, but $5.4 bn of it depends on building the Piketon plant; Q3 revenue -26% and an adjusted loss [S10].
- Reactors: Japanese restarts are happening (Kashiwazaki-Kariwa 6 back in commercial operation, Apr 2026; worth about JPY 100 bn a year to TEPCO for units 6 and 7) [S11].
  SMRs: Bank of America expects real use from 2030 at the earliest and widely only 2035-40; Oklo and NuScale keep issuing new shares [S13]. They are -76% and -81% in a year [Y].
- Early or late: the stocks fell in 2026 (Cameco -44% from its January high, BWXT -50%, Centrus -61% in a year) while the uranium price rose. That is a reset, not a boom. But earnings are not speeding up: Cameco's 9-month uranium EBITDA was flat ($855 m vs $862 m) [S9].
- Priced in: Kazatomprom (P/E about 12 on 2025 profit) needs about 1%/yr: cheap, because of Kazakhstan state and tax risk and a Rosatom partner. BWXT (fwd 22.4) needs 14%/yr. Cameco (fwd 39.2) needs 27%/yr: still expensive.
- What kills it: uranium term price below $75; utilities delaying contracts; Kazakhstan raising its mining tax; US waivers for Russian enriched uranium; SMR orders cancelled or not financed; no US funding for HALEU.
- Base rate (uranium, 2007 top, 5 leaders) [Y]: Cameco took 16 years to regain its May 2007 high (Jul 2023). Paladin is -92%, Denison -77%, Energy Fuels -97%. Uranium Energy is about flat (listed at the top). 0 of 5 multiplied from the top.

  Already past the top: Seagate -53%, Western Digital -62%, Sandisk -63% from their 15 Jun 2026 highs [Y].
  Low forward P/Es (Sandisk 2.8, Western Digital 7.0, Seagate 7.6) price in an 8-25%/yr fall in EPS: the usual sign of a peaking memory cycle.
- Names under 0.3 are not storage plays. Hoya (0.10) makes glass platters for hard drives, but grows mainly in chip mask blanks; one 100-share lot costs about Rs 13.6 L. Resonac (0.29) makes chemicals with chip materials, P/E 46, net debt JPY 527 bn. Nidec (0.23) has an accounting probe.
- What would reopen it: a 50%+ fall in memory prices with storage shares near their 2024 levels and corr back under 0.3. Not expected soon.
- Base rate (solar 2008 and hydrogen/EV 2021 tops, 10 leaders): Suntech, LDK and Q-Cells went bankrupt [S16]. First Solar regained its 2008 high only in May 2026 (18 years) and is -44% again [Y].
  Plug -98%, Ballard -96%, Nel -94%, ITM -87% [Y]. Nikola and Proterra are bankrupt; most 2021 EV SPACs lost 80-100% [S17]. 0 of 10 multiplied from the top.
