# India special situations and safe-money routes: checked (2026-11-15)

## Summary
- Do first: when FD money that must stay safe comes free (PGB maturities; HDFC 10 May 2027), put Rs 5-10 L in listed tax-free PSU bonds (5.25-5.78% tax-free, about 8.2-9.0% before tax) and keep the rest in Kotak Arbitrage (5.10% / 5.71% after tax). Both are safe-money choices outside the 5% idea cap.
- Buyback tenders still pay after tax: from 1 Apr 2026 they are capital gains again, not deemed dividend. Bajaj Auto 2026 gave +8.6% after tax in 3 weeks. Wipro 2026 lost 1.5%. Each tender is a Rs 2 L position, which the trading rules (Rs 40k per position, paper trading only until about mid-Jan 2027) do not allow yet. You decide whether to add an "event trade" rule.
- IPOs: apply only for one minimum retail lot. sNII (Rs 2 L+) adds little once you count the interest lost on the blocked money. Mahanadi Coalfields: band Rs 129-136 (16 Nov), above the about Rs 120 skip line: SKIP.
- Drop: SLB lending (none of your holdings are in demand), open offers (no small-shareholder quota), T-bills and G-secs (4.2-4.5% after tax, below arbitrage), discounted G-secs (only +0.1-0.2 pp).

### 2.1 Tax-free bonds (rank 1)
- Amount: Rs 5-10 L of the FD money that stays safe after the plan's moves (`research/themes/cash-and-fds.md` keeps
  about Rs 6.7 L of FD money safe, plus the Rs 6 L emergency money, which stays liquid).
- Money source and timing: each PGB FD as it matures (dates still unknown), or the HDFC FDs on 10 May 2027. Pick bonds
  maturing 2029-2031. Use limit orders in the NSE/BSE retail series and check the last trade date, because many series trade rarely.
- Edge: at 5.78% tax-free it beats arbitrage under 12 months (5.10%) and roughly equals it after 12 months (5.71%). The return
  is fixed to maturity and does not use the Rs 1.25 L LTCG exemption. The premium you pay (Rs 118 on Rs 1,000) is
  already counted in the YTM; whether it also gives a capital loss is tax question 2.

### 2.2 Buyback tenders (rank 3): worked examples, small-shareholder quota, one PAN
Buy the day before the record date (T+1 settlement). The small-shareholder limit is measured at the record-date close.
Costs: about 0.1% STT on each side plus about Rs 200 brokerage and charges. Prices: Yahoo NSE closes (`.venv` yfinance, 15 Nov 2026).

| If the returned shares had sold at the record-date close | +Rs 12,440 (+6.2%) | - |

- Rule of thumb from three large tenders (Infosys 2025 55%, Bajaj 50.5%, Wipro 35.1% accepted in the small quota): you
  sell only 35-55% at the buyback price, and the rest is a 2-3 week bet on the stock. Wipro's 22% premium was not enough
  because the price fell 12% after the record date. Small entitlements (Genus Power 2026: 4.12%, 1 in 25) are almost all stock risk.
- Realistic return: about +2-4% after tax a tender, if you take only tenders with a premium above about 15% and an
  entitlement above about 20%. At 8-12 such tenders a year that is about Rs 30k-1 L after tax per PAN (estimate, not a dataset).
- Fit: Rs 2 L in one stock is 100% of the trading budget. The trading rules cap a position at Rs 40k and a loss at Rs 2,000,
  allow real money only after the paper check, and exclude the futures hedge. You decide: add an "event trade" rule
  (for example one tender at a time, Rs 2 L, no stop), or paper-trade the next 2-3 tenders first. Next on the calendar: Global
  Education (record date 18 Nov 2026, Rs 162; entitlement not yet seen).

### 2.4 Which bucket each route uses
- Not ideas (outside the Rs 21 L cap, of which Rs 20.75 L is used): tax-free bonds, arbitrage, T-bills and G-secs are safe-money
  or cash-management choices inside the 15-25% safe band; SLB uses shares you already hold.
- Event trades (buybacks, IPOs, open offers) are short-term trades, not long-term ideas. They belong to the Rs 2 L trading budget
  and its rules, not to the idea cap. That budget is paper-only until the paper check passes.

## 2a. Retail rules (finance-legal, C23-C28 in `legal/compliance/india-retail-rules.md`, 2026-11-15)
| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Buyback small-shareholder quota | One PAN counts once. The Rs 2 L limit is checked at the record-date close; 15% of the buyback is reserved for small shareholders. Demat accounts are combined when the holder PANs are in the same order; a joint account with your spouse is not combined with your own. Open-market buybacks (back since 1 Aug 2026) have no small-shareholder quota |
| IPO retail | A bid of up to Rs 2 L; allotment is by lot draw. Shareholders of the parent company and employees may also bid in the retail category |
| Family accounts | Each member needs their own PAN, demat and bank/UPI; a bid paid from someone else's account is rejected. An outright gift of money is fine (the income is clubbed with yours, tax row 34); do not operate their accounts. Benami law: spouse and child are exempt if the money comes from known sources |
| Tax-free bonds | Buy on NSE/BSE through a broker or a SEBI-registered online bond platform; avoid informal deals between individuals |
