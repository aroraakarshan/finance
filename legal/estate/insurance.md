# Life insurance and employer benefits

## Summary
A term policy on the user's life exists, paid by his father; its owner, cover and nominee are
unknown. If the father owns it, the claim goes to the father (or his estate), not to the user's
heirs, and cannot pay the US estate tax. Ask the father for the policy details; if he owns it,
an absolute assignment to the user (s.38) lets the user nominate. Whether total cover is enough is
the user's decision. Also check the employer group life and gratuity nominees.

## Nomination and assignment rules
- Insurance Act 1938 s.39 as substituted by the Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act 2015 (No. 5 of
  2015, in force from 26 Dec 2014): s.39(7) where the policyholder nominates parents, spouse or
  children, they are "beneficially entitled" (not trustees); s.39(9) creditors keep their right;
  s.39(4) an assignment under s.38 cancels the nomination.
  https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2015/Insurance_Act,_2015_0.pdf
- s.39(1) (2015 text): "The holder of a policy of life insurance on his own life may ...
  nominate". s.38(1): assignment, with or without consideration, by endorsement on the policy or
  a separate instrument signed by the assignor and attested by at least one witness; it binds the
  insurer only after written notice (s.38(5)); unless stated as conditional it is absolute and
  the assignee is recognised as entitled to the benefit (s.38(8) and Explanation). Same PDF.
- 2025: the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act 2025 (No. 40 of 2025,
  assent 20 Dec 2025, in force on dates to be notified) does not amend s.38 or s.39 (searched the
  Act text; it adds record-keeping of nominations by insurers).
  https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2025/Sabka_Bima_Sabki_Raksha_(Amendment_of_Insurance_Laws)_Act_2025.pdf
  Its commencement notifications: not verified.

## Term policy paid by the father
User info via finance-main, 2026-11-15. Who is the policyholder decides everything; paying the
premium alone does not make the father the owner.

| Who is proposer / policyholder | Who gets the claim on the user's death | Can the user nominate? | If the father dies first |
|---|---|---|---|
| The user (father only pays) | The user's nominee; a parent, spouse or child nominee is beneficially entitled (s.39(7)) | Yes, and change it (s.39(1)-(2)) | No effect on the policy |
| The father, on the son's life | The father as owner (not a policy "on his own life", so no s.39 nomination by the user) | No; the father can move ownership to the user by absolute assignment (s.38), then the user nominates | Policy passes under the father's will or personal law (his heirs, not necessarily the user's) |

- Not verified: insurer and IRDAI rules on a proposer's "nominee"/successor for a policy on
  another person's life, and whether insurers issue term cover on an adult earning son with the
  father as proposer (insurable interest). Gift-tax effect of the assignment: `finance-tax`.
- Ask the father: insurer, policy number (last 4 only in files), sum assured, term and expiry
  date, premium amount and payer, proposer/policyholder vs life assured, nominee(s).

## Is cover enough
User decision; no product advice here. finance-research suggests about Rs 1 Cr in total,
including the US estate tax exposure (about $87.7k / Rs 82.5 L if unvested Microsoft awards vest
at death, about $46k without; `research/themes/us-estate-tax.md`). Proceeds paid to the father
would not be available to the user's executor to pay that tax.
