# Indian buybacks, IPOs, family applications and tax-free bonds (SEBI rules)

## Summary
- Buybacks: yes, one PAN is one small shareholder. SEBI Buy-back reg 9(ix) clubs all demat accounts whose holder PANs are in the same order. The Rs 2 L limit uses the record-date close. A joint account (you + spouse) is a different holder sequence and is not clubbed with your sole account.
- IPOs: retail (RII) means a bid of at most Rs 2 L. Use one bid per category, from your own bank account or UPI. Third-party bank or UPI bids are rejected. You may also bid in the shareholder quota (up to Rs 2 L) or the employee quota and in the retail category (ICDR reg 33(2)(c)).
- Family: each person bids or tenders in their own name, demat, bank and UPI, with money that is theirs (an outright gift is fine), and decides and places their own orders. Do not run their accounts. SEBI calls an account "effectively controlled by another person" a mule account (PFUTP, 2024).
- Tax-free bonds: buy on NSE/BSE through your broker, or from a SEBI-registered online bond platform (OBPP) that settles through the clearing corporation. Avoid informal OTC deals with other individuals: every OTC trade must be reported and cleared, and you get no settlement guarantee.
- To do: none before the next trade. Gifts to the family go by bank transfer with a short gift note. Tax (clubbing, buyback and bond tax) is in `finance-tax`'s [tax lines](../../tax/global-actions-2026-11.md) (rows 32-34). Questions [C23-C28](../questions.md#indian-buybacks-ipos-and-bonds).

## (a) Buyback small-shareholder quota and IPO retail quota
| Rule | Text (short) | Reg / para | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small shareholder | Market value of holding at the record-date close, on the exchange with the highest volume, "not more than two lakh rupee" | Buy-back reg 2(1)(n) | R1 |
| 15% reservation (tender offer) | 15% of the buyback size, or the small holders' entitlement if higher, is reserved for small shareholders | reg 6 proviso | R1 |
| Clubbing across demat accounts | "Holdings of multiple demat accounts would be clubbed together for identification of small shareholder if sequence of Permanent Account Number for all holders is matching" (physical folios: same sequence of joint-holder names) | reg 9(ix) Explanation | R1 |
| Entitlement | Shares are accepted against the record-date entitlement in each category; any leftover goes first to holders in the same category who tendered extra | reg 9(viii)-(x) | R1 |
| Tender through the exchange | Tendering and settlement go through the stock exchange mechanism | reg 9(vii) | R1 |
| Open-market buybacks | Phased down from 2023, barred from 1 Apr 2025 to 31 Jul 2026, allowed again from 1 Aug 2026 (each less than 15% of paid-up capital and free reserves). There is no small-shareholder quota; the company buys at the market price | reg 4(iv)(b) provisos; reg 17(ii)-(iii) (Amendment Regs 2026, in force 1 Aug 2026) | R1 |
| Latest amendment | Regulations amended up to 6 Jul 2026 | R1 header | R1 |
| Buyback tax | Deemed dividend from 1 Oct 2024; capital gains again from 1 Apr 2026 | finance-tax row 33 | R8 |
| Retail individual investor (RII) | Individual who bids "for a value of not more than two lakhs rupees" | ICDR reg 2(1)(vv) | R2 |
| RII share and allotment | At least 35% of the net offer goes to RIIs (book building). Each allottee gets at least one minimum bid lot, so oversubscribed issues are a lot draw. Minimum bid Rs 10,000-15,000 | ICDR reg 32(1)(a), 49(4), 47(2); Sch XIV Part A | R2 |
| Small NII (sNII) | One third of the NII portion is for bids of Rs 2 L-10 L | ICDR reg 32(3A)(a) | R2 |
| Shareholder and employee quotas | Shareholders of a listed subsidiary or listed promoter company may get up to 10% of the issue; employees get up to Rs 2 L allotted (Rs 5 L if undersubscribed). Other quota bidders cannot also bid in the net offer, except "an employee and retail individual shareholder" | ICDR reg 33(1)-(2), 2(1)(ww) | R2 |
| One bid per account / UPI ID | "do not submit more than one Bid cum Application Form per ASBA Account" / "for each UPI ID"; an employee-quota bid plus a net-offer bid is not a multiple bid | RHP Offer Procedure, Don'ts 16-17; notes to the Offer Structure | R4 |
| Same PAN = multiple bid | Bids under the same PAN in one category are treated as multiple bids and rejected (the registrar checks by PAN) | General Information Document (circular SEBI/HO/CFD/DIL1/CIR/P/2020/37, 17 Mar 2020) | not verified (GID not opened; the RHP applies the same-PAN test only to FPIs) |
| UPI limit | Individuals bidding up to Rs 5 L must use UPI | ICDR Master Circular, Ch 10 (as quoted in the RHP) | R3, R4 |

## (b) Applying in family members' names
| Rule | Text (short) | Reg / section | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Own bank account or UPI only | The investor "shall use only his / her own bank account or only his/her own bank account linked UPI ID". Third-party bank or UPI bids "are liable for rejection"; the registrar rejects them | ICDR Master Circular Ch 10 para 8.1-8.4 | R3 |
| Own PAN and demat | PAN, DP ID and client ID are validated live with the depository | ICDR MC Ch 10 para 7.1 | R3 |
| Fictitious or multiple applications | Penal: applying, or helping someone apply, "in different names or in different combinations of his/her name" | Companies Act 2013 s.38(1), quoted in ICDR Sch VI | R2 (s.38 text as quoted; Act and penalty s.447 not verified) |
| Mule accounts | A "mule account" is a trading, demat or linked bank account "in the name(s) of a person, where the account is effectively controlled by another person, whether or not the consideration ... [is] paid by such other person". Dealing through mule accounts to manipulate is fraud | PFUTP reg 2(1)(b)(i), 2(1)(da), 4(1) Expl (ii) (inserted 1 Jul 2024) | R5 |
| Fraud in an issue | No person shall use any device or scheme to defraud in connection with an issue of listed securities | PFUTP reg 3(b)-(d), 4(1) | R5 |
| Benami | Property held in one person's name, paid for by another and held for the payer's benefit, is benami. Exceptions: property in a spouse's or child's name paid from the individual's known sources; for a brother, sister, parent or other lineal relative, only if the individual is a joint owner | Benami Act 1988 (as amended 2016) s.2(9)(A)(b)(iii)-(iv) | R6 |
| Minors | Minors with a valid demat account may bid ("other than minors having valid depository accounts") | RHP Offer Procedure, Don't 13 | R4; minor's own bank or UPI: not verified |
| Past IPO cases | 2003-05 IPO scam: SEBI orders against people who used thousands of benami demat accounts to grab retail allotments | SEBI orders 2006 | not verified |

Plain answer:
| Case | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Spouse, adult child or parent applies with their own money, own PAN, demat, bank and UPI, and decides and places the order | Allowed: a separate investor |
| Same, but with money you gave them as an outright gift (bank transfer, no promise to return the shares or profit) | Allowed under SEBI and benami rules as read. Spouse: income is clubbed with yours (finance-tax row 34) |
| You pay from your account, use your UPI, or run their app and orders | Rejected (third-party payment), and their accounts look like mule accounts |
| Shares or profits come back to you (they hold for your benefit) | Benami risk for parents and siblings, unless you are a joint holder; a PFUTP and s.38 risk if done at scale |
| Splitting your own holdings into accounts with a different holder order to stay under Rs 2 L | Allowed by the clubbing rule's wording, but grey: ask a lawyer (C23) |

## (c) Tax-free bonds: exchange vs OTC
| Rule | Text (short) | Reg / para | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| OTC trades reported | "All OTC trades in non-convertible securities shall be reported" on an exchange reporting platform (NSE, BSE, MSEI) within 15 minutes | NCS Master Circular Ch XVI para 1.1 | R7 |
| Who reports | "all person(s) dealing in such securities irrespective of whether they are SEBI registered intermediaries or otherwise" | Ch XVI para 1.3 | R7 |
| Clearing | "All trades in non-convertible securities shall necessarily be cleared and settled" through NSCCL, ICCL or MCCIL | Ch XVI para 1.5 | R7 |
| RFQ platform | For regulated entities, listed companies and institutional investors; not open to individuals directly | Ch XVI para 1.2; Ch XXII | R7 |
| Online bond platforms (OBPP) | Regulated since Nov 2022 (notification of 9 Nov 2022, NCS reg 51A). Must register as a stock broker (debt segment). Listed bonds only; every order routed through the exchange RFQ and settled through the clearing corporation | Ch XXI paras 5, 5.1-5.2, 3.4.1 | R7 |
| Trading lot | Bonds from public issues (most tax-free retail series) trade in their face-value units (often Rs 1,000). The Ch V face-value rule covers only private placements | Ch V para 2.3, 3 | R7 |
| Off-market transfer between individuals (DIS) | Possible at the depository, but the trade is still an OTC trade (report and clear). Stamp duty 0.0001% on debenture transfers since 1 Jul 2020 | Indian Stamp Act (Finance Act 2019) | not verified |
| Tax | Interest exempt under 1961 s.10(15)(iv)(h), kept by the 2025 Act; premium over face value is a capital loss | finance-tax row 32 | R8 |

## Risks
| Risk | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Buyback: record-date value just above Rs 2 L | You fall into the general category (lower acceptance); price moves before the record date |
| IPO: duplicate bids or a third-party UPI | All bids can be rejected; money stays blocked until unblocked |
| Family accounts you control | Mule account under PFUTP; SEBI can bar and impound; Companies Act s.38 is penal |
| Benami (parents, siblings, held for you) | Confiscation and prosecution under the Benami Act; keep gifts outright and documented |
| Informal OTC bond deal | No clearing guarantee (you can pay and not get the bonds); reporting breach; mispricing |
